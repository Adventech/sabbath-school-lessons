**Read This Week’s Passage:** 1 Kings 19:1–16

### A Spiritual GPS

The Global Positioning System (GPS) helps travelers know where they are, where they’re going, and even provides warnings about hazards. Spiritual travelers journeying to heaven also need guidance from a heavenly “positioning system.” One way God did this in times past was through the ministry of Old Testament prophets. In their narratives, counsels, prophecies, poetry, and songs, we discover much about the dozens of prophets who presented God’s messages to the people.

The stories of the Old Testament prophets help us understand the gift of prophecy as it was manifested by some of these servants of God. These prophets were people like us in that they were frail human beings subject to fear, anger, and discouragement. Life for them was not easy but filled with challenges and hardships, as it is for all of us.

One of the preeminent prophets of the Old Testament is Elijah, a man who never died but was ultimately translated to heaven in a chariot of fire (2 Kings 2:11). Centuries later, Elijah was widely recognized as an icon among the Old Testament prophets. People speculated about whether John the Baptist and Jesus were Elijah (John 1:21; Mark 8:27, 28). When Jesus was hanging on the cross, the crowds taunted Him for not being delivered by Elijah (Matt. 27:49).

Like a spiritual GPS, Elijah was called to orient the nation of Israel to their true spiritual condition and help them identify the spiritual hazards endangering them. In 1 Kings 17, we read the story of how God sent Elijah to King Ahab to announce a multiyear drought that would call the nation to account for its apostasy. God used this affliction to turn His people away from idolatry and back to obedience. The entire nation blamed Elijah for their suffering and sought his death. To save his own life, Elijah fled to the mountains and hid from those sent by the king to hunt him down. Finally, Elijah confronted King Ahab and called the whole nation to Mount Carmel, where he confronted the prophets of Baal and Asherah. God’s messenger made a Spirit-filled appeal to idolatrous Israel to denounce their false gods and return to the Lord. The people knew their choices had led them into national apostasy. They were now ready to embrace Elijah’s God again, who had proved to be superior to their false gods—which in reality didn’t even exist.

Just prior to Christ’s second coming, God commands His church to proclaim Elijah’s message again and for people to turn away from their “idols” and be ready to stand in the great day of the Lord (Mal. 4:5, 6). God still communicates with His people through channels He has selected. His Word and the gift of prophecy provide admonition, warning, counsel, and encouragement.

Elijah’s message, a call to return to God with our whole hearts, is one that’s needed again today. “A revival of true godliness among us is the greatest and most urgent of all our needs. To seek this should be our first work. There must be earnest effort to obtain the blessing of the Lord, not because God is not willing to bestow His blessing upon us, but because we are unprepared to receive it.”<sup>1</sup> According to Malachi 4:6, this end-time call is not just a theoretical message but one that results in true healing of people’s hearts and homes (Mal. 4:6). The family is the cornerstone of the church and an indispensable building block of community. Revival in the home spreads a heavenly love to the church, the community, and the nation, and it motivates God’s people for mission. This is God’s purpose for the Christian home.

Without prophets like Elijah, God’s people in the Old Testament would have drifted off course forever, unchallenged in their sins and uncertain what God expected of them. The prophets called God’s people back to the right path and guided them in the way of truth.
