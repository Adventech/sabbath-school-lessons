### Contending with False Prophets

Elijah’s message stood in daring opposition to the messages of hundreds of false prophets who were funded by the royal family (1 Kings 18:19). This opposition was not uncommon; Old Testament prophets often contended with competing messages from false prophets. There is evidence of false prophets even in Israel’s early history (Deut. 18:20). Fortunately, God gave His people guidance on how to tell who His true prophets were. As recipients of God’s revelation and inspiration, true prophets faithfully, accurately, and honestly transmitted God’s messages, even if at times this set them in direct conflict with the people in power.

When true prophets and false prophets shared conflicting messages at the same time, the people had to choose which voice to believe. Such was the dilemma in 1 Kings 22, when Ahab, king of Israel, counseled with Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, regarding whether to fight against Ramoth Gilead (1 Kings 22:3, 4). After a group of four hundred “prophets” unanimously advised the kings to fight (1 Kings 22:6), Jehoshaphat, distrustful of these prophets of Asherah, insisted that he and Ahab specifically consult “a prophet of the Lord” (1 Kings 22:7).

When a man named Micaiah, known to be a prophet of God but hated by Ahab (1 Kings 22:8), was summoned, the messenger who brought him urged him not to speak any message that contradicted what had already been presented (1 Kings 22:13). The pressure to lie that Micaiah faced was likely the same pressure the false prophets of Asherah felt to say whatever the king desired. But Micaiah did not acquiesce. Instead, he waited for God to tell him what to say (1 Kings 22:14).

To have a king say of this true prophet, “I hate him, because he does not prophesy good concerning me, but evil” (1 Kings 22:8), says a lot about the king, but just as much about the prophet. He was obviously a man not afraid to speak the truth, even to the king himself—not a way to win royal favor, to say the least.

Micaiah stood alone against four hundred false prophets as he presented the truth revealed to him. Even when facing pressure to alter messages of judgment from the Lord to placate the evil King Ahab, Micaiah presented God’s truth, regardless of how it would be received.

No matter what God does, Satan has counterfeits. Israel faced both true and false prophets. The key was knowing the difference.
