### The Prophets’ Humanity

The Lord used Elijah to do a tremendous work in Israel despite fervent opposition. What the Lord accomplished through him was incredible, yet Elijah had problems and failures. His biography is an inspiring story of courage and faithfulness, but it’s not free from stumbles and setbacks. He was a man who struggled “with a nature like ours” (James 5:17).

Elijah had a stunning success on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18). God had worked in a mighty way through him to perform one of the most astonishing miracles depicted in Old Testament history: at the sound of Elijah’s words, fire literally came down from heaven (1 Kings 18:38)! You’d expect this man of God to be filled with euphoria after such an experience. However, the pinnacle of Elijah’s public ministry was immediately followed by a dark time of deep despair.

Threatened by a wicked queen, Elijah suddenly panicked and ran for his life. Forgetting how the Lord had just protected and guided him, Elijah became controlled by fear. In terrible distress, he exclaimed, “It is enough! Now, Lord, take my life, for I am no better than my fathers!” (1 Kings 19:4). This sounds like a man suffering from depression. But why?

What happened on Mount Carmel would certainly have been a difficult act to follow, but something else must’ve been going on here. Perhaps the key appears in his words, “I have been very zealous for the Lord God of hosts; for the children of Israel have forsaken Your covenant, torn down Your altars, and killed Your prophets with the sword. I alone am left; and they seek to take my life” (1 Kings 19:10). Elijah felt terrible loneliness and lamented his apparent failures in ministry. Apostasy remained, despite his best efforts—as if he alone could have corrected the entire nation. Whatever the case was, we can see the humanity and fragility of this man of God. In other words, Elijah truly had “a nature like ours.”

The story of Elijah reminds us that even for followers of God, not every day is a great day! Elijah was not God’s prophet because he was perfect. God worked through him and spoke through him despite his weaknesses. Like Elijah, all of us have our low points. The important thing is to remember God’s faithfulness and not give up.

**Questions to consider:**

`How did God seek, heal, and restore Elijah when he was at a very low point? What does this tell you about God’s care and mercy?`

`When have you struggled most with a sense of failure in your life or endeavors? What Bible promises can you claim to help you work through discouragement?`
