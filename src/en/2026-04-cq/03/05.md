### Functions of Prophets

The function of Old Testament prophets included the often difficult responsibility of delivering messages to kings. The prophet Samuel tried to spare Israel from being under a king, but the people demanded it. They wanted “a king to judge us like all the nations” (1 Sam. 8:5). What a sad but powerful example of the danger God’s people have faced in every age: the desire to be like those around them. In this case, they had no idea what this request would bring, even though God forewarned them through His prophet (1 Sam. 8:11–18). Based on their desires, and despite God’s warning to them, Samuel anointed Israel’s first king, known as King Saul.

The Old Testament books of 1 and 2 Kings and 1 and 2 Chronicles record the type and quality of rulership exhibited by the kings of Israel and Judah. They show that the kings who did evil far outnumbered those who did good in God’s sight. One of the core functions of the prophets was to help lead unfaithful kings back to obedience to God’s law. As a result, these prophets often faced threats, with some even harmed and killed by kings who led God’s people away from Him (1 Kings 18:4–19; 2 Chron. 24:20–22).

Prophets also interacted with priests. Moses, a prophet of God, anointed Aaron and his sons as priests of Israel (Lev. 8:10–13). At times, God sent words of rebuke to priests through the prophets (Hos. 6:9; Mal. 2:1–3). One striking example is when the entire household of Eli was confronted for their corrupt practices (1 Sam. 2:27–36). Rebukes such as this one were very strong, for they were given in response to rampant corruption among people given special privileges.

Prophets were teachers of the people (2 Chron. 34:14–28). They presented messages from God to the priests, kings, and the nation at large (Jer. 5:31; Ezek. 22:26). Prophets also spoke to surrounding nations to outline God’s plan for them, a theme repeated several times in the messages of various Old Testament prophets (Isa. 60:1–3; 62:1, 2). Prophets were expected not only to faithfully pass on the messages God gave them but were sometimes also instructed to write them down and preserve them (2 Chron. 13:22). Again, no matter how human and therefore frail the prophets were, they were men and women of prayer, the Word, and service to God and His people (Jer. 42:4; 2 Kings 22:15–17).
