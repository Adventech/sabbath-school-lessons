As we journey through this study, it’s important to engage deeply with the material and reflect on its implications for our faith and daily lives. In your Sabbath School class (or Bible study group), use the outline of questions below to lead a thoughtful discussion based on this week’s main passage and supporting verses.

**The prophets’ humanity:**

1. What kind of setback did the prophet Elijah suffer immediately after his victory on Mount Carmel? 1 Kings 19:1–10
2. What kind of human frailties did the prophet Elijah battle? James 5:17

`Personal reflection: Why did Elijah feel so alone? What are some ways we can cope when we feel alone?`

**Contending with false prophets:**

1. How many false prophets opposed Elijah and his message? 1 Kings 18:19
2. Why did King Ahab favor the false prophets’ message over that of the true prophet? How must true prophets respond when pressured to make their message conform? 1 Kings 22:1–14

`Personal reflection: How can we resist the trap of listening only to the voices that please us?`

**The role and nature of prophets:**

1. Are there instances when God has used female prophets to guide His people? Exod. 15:20; Judg. 4:4
2. Who did King Josiah and Hilkiah the priest turn to for spiritual guidance? What does this say about the relationship between prophets, priests, and kings? 2 Kings 22:11–14
3. Were Old Testament prophets ever required to challenge corruption in the priesthood? 1 Sam. 2:27–36; Mal. 2:1–3

`Personal reflection: Imagine being an Old Testament prophet. What would you like about the calling, and what part of your responsibility would you dread?`

**Key takeaways to remember:**

- Prophets were not perfect people. They struggled with normal human challenges and frailties.
- False prophets’ messages are usually more popular than true prophets’ messages.
- God has called both men and women to be prophets.
