**Read This Week’s Passage:** Isaiah 6

### A Difficult Calling

In biblical times, prophets often played key roles in the history of God’s people. Sometimes they were center stage, taking powerful, if not crucial, positions in the life of the community. However, being a prophet was a difficult calling. Often, their lives were not easy and were filled with sorrow, rejection, misunderstanding, and frustration. They often did not readily accept their call to the prophetic office—and no wonder. Many prophets were strongly opposed by those who should have welcomed their messages. Some of God’s prophets were ignored, ridiculed, beaten, or jailed. Others were stoned or hunted like wild animals. Several were martyred.

Not all of the prophets were called by God to warn of impending doom. However, all were called to restore the people to faithfulness and often warned against apostasy, the oppression of those who were poor and needy, and the violation of the covenant and its stipulations. Most important of all, however, the prophets were to reveal the promise of salvation found in the Messiah. Today, in a world flooded with violence, greed, immorality, exploitation, and all manner of satanic influences, the prophetic voice is often rejected or simply ignored. We are called to heed the voice of God’s prophets and to be messengers of the hope given to us through those prophets. The enduring testimony of the biblical prophets and of God’s Spirit in our post-apostolic era calls us to recognize and to warn others of the impending crisis that will come upon the world, yet central to that warning is the good news of redemption for Jesus’ faithful people—those who have remained in covenantal relationship with Him (Rev. 12:17).

Last week, we learned that in Eden after the Fall, God worked to reestablish communication with human beings. This week, we will examine some monumental times in history when God called prophets to realign His people with His messages for a fallen world.

We will begin with the prophetic calling of Abraham. Years after the dispersion from Babel, God chose Abram (later Abraham) to be an example of covenant faith, not just to his family but for all future generations. When Abram was surrounded by apostasy and moral ambivalence, God called him to leave his country, his parents, and his friends and to follow Him into unknown territory.

God appeared to Abraham and communicated the terms of the covenant. He showed him how humanity’s salvation would be accomplished through Christ’s sacrifice (Gen. 15:6–17). To test Abraham’s faith and to foretell His Son’s sacrifice, God called Abraham to offer Isaac, his own son, as a sacrifice to Him (Gen. 22:1–17). What an excruciating test! Think about all the reasons Abraham could have disregarded God’s explicit command, yet he faithfully obeyed. Ever merciful and good, God stopped Abraham from killing Isaac and provided a ram in his stead.

Abraham’s life and ministry centered on having complete faith in God. Though he was, of course, a flawed man, he learned to believe God’s promises and to trust God’s leading no matter what was happening (Gen. 15:6; Gal. 3:6–9; Heb. 11:8). His life is an example of a believer placing full confidence in the Lord.

There are many parallels between Abraham’s day and ours. As paganism and idolatry ran rampant in Abraham’s day, so today many of the world’s competing ideologies aim to eliminate or marginalize Christianity. In contrast with much of the prevailing culture, God’s people today are called to reflect His image faithfully in our daily interactions with others.
