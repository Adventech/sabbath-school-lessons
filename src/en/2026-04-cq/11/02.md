### During the Time of the Judges

After delivering the Israelites from slavery in Egypt, God gave them the land of Canaan. He promised to bless them abundantly if they followed His laws and commandments. He also warned that breaking their covenant with Him would result in suffering and destruction (Deut. 27–30). Following the death of Joshua, the Israelites turned away from keeping the covenant. They were then oppressed by the neighboring peoples and cried out to God. In response, “the Lord raised up judges who delivered them out of the hand of those who plundered them” (Judg. 2:16). He also sent prophets to encourage the Israelites to renew their covenant with God and to empower them against their oppressors.

At a time when Israel was threatened by nearby enemies, God called Deborah to be a prophetess and a judge who would encourage and strengthen the people. She played a pivotal part in relaying God’s messages to Israel and Barak, the nation’s commander, as he rallied the troops. It was Deborah who gave Barak the signal to begin the battle, stating, “Up! For this is the day in which the Lord has delivered Sisera into your hand. Has not the Lord gone out before you?” (Judg. 4:14). Her prophetic words gave assurance to the leaders and the people that despite the risks, it was time to take action.

Deborah’s leadership and her faith in God’s promise of victory strengthened the Israelites in their fight against their enemies. Barak’s skill as a war commander and his willingness, however reluctant, to lead the army of Israel was also important, but that’s not what won the battle. This is perhaps the most important message of the story of the prophet Deborah and of the soldier Barak. In the middle of celebrating a stunning victory, the most powerful people in Israel—a judge and a victorious commander—led the community to glorify God. This is the nature of true spiritual leadership.

Deborah’s prophetic ministry testifies to God’s faithfulness in times of distress and oppression. When God gave her the message for Barak, she did not question the feasibility of summoning enough troops, nor did she argue that the Israelites were too weak to win. Instead, she relayed her message with conviction and encouraged Barak to set aside his doubts and to trust God’s providence. Her influence inspired Barak and his armies to move forward in faith.
