**Read This Week’s Passage:** Judges 4, 5

### Revealing His Plans

Since the Fall, human beings have been predisposed to choose the pleasures of sin, which lasts only for a time, over eternal life in Jesus. God knows this sad truth, but He loves us and works for our salvation anyway.

This reality is why, at crucial junctures in history, God has acted in remarkable ways through the prophetic office on His people’s behalf. However, when His efforts have been repeatedly refused, He warned of impending judgment. God reveals His plans through prophets. “Surely the Lord God does nothing, Unless He reveals His secret to His servants the prophets” (Amos 3:7). Through His prophets, the Lord always communicates His actions before He does them. If people repent, He is quick to forgive. He continues to communicate with us in the same way today. It’s reasonable, therefore, that God should speak to His people in earth’s last days, just as He spoke to His people in centuries past.

Beginning with the early history of the world, God made every effort to reach humanity. Even after the Fall, He sought a relationship with Adam and Eve, and for a time the inhabitants of earth called “on the name of the Lord” (Gen. 4:26). As generations passed, the number of those who walked with God decreased. Those who continued to follow God testified to His goodness, condemned the sins of those around them, and warned them of the consequences of their actions.

By Noah’s time, however, the inhabitants of the earth were so wicked that God chose to destroy them (Gen. 6:5–7). Noah, a preacher of righteousness (2 Pet. 2:5), no doubt warned all who would listen about the destruction to come. God showed Noah what was coming and gave him instructions for building a lifeboat, the ark, to save all who would heed his warnings and repent (Heb. 11:7).

The Flood was a pivotal moment in human history, and it’s referenced repeatedly in both the Old and New Testaments as an example of the depths of human depravity and the height of God’s amazing grace. God’s righteousness is manifested in His judgment against a sinful creation and in His deliverance of all who accept salvation.

Amid the prevailing corruption, Methuselah, Noah, and many others labored to keep alive the knowledge of the true God and to stay the tide of moral evil. A hundred and twenty years before the Flood, the Lord by a holy angel declared to Noah His purpose, and directed him to build an ark. While building the ark he was to preach that God would bring a flood of water upon the earth to destroy the wicked. Those who would believe the message, and would prepare for that event by repentance and reformation, should find pardon and be saved.<sup>1</sup>

God not only told Noah about the Flood and rescued the occupants of the ark but also established a covenant with Noah and all creation, promising never to destroy the world again through water (Gen. 9:8–17). Every rainbow contains the promise and hope of redemption.

As God sent Noah to call people to repentance and announce judgment, so He continued sending prophets to instruct His people. During the time of the judges, God used prophets to guide and encourage Israel. God spoke through prophets during the time of the kings to call them to faithfulness and to warn of coming judgment. God sent prophets during the Babylonian exile to encourage the people and guide their return to their homeland. God called prophets in the New Testament to establish gospel teachings as well as to guide the organization and mission of the church. In the last days, prophets once again play an important role (Joel 2:28–31).

This week’s study looks back at crucial times in salvation history when God used prophets to make His will clear to His people and to the wider world. We will also explore the role of Ellen G. White’s inspired ministry at pivotal times in the growth of the Adventist movement.
