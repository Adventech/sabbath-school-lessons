### A Prophet for the Time of Waiting

In the New Testament, God continued to use the prophetic gift to teach and guide His people. The Holy Spirit inspired Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John to write their gospel accounts for the benefit of the entire world. Other books of the New Testament include letters that Paul and other apostles wrote to churches and believers. The apostle John wrote the last book of the Bible to communicate important prophecies that Jesus delivered to him through an angel (Rev. 1:1, 2). John was instructed to write the things God showed him so that all who read could be blessed and understand future events (Rev. 1:3, 11, 19).

Early Christians eagerly anticipated Christ’s promised return. When Jesus rose in the clouds, John had heard the angel explain that He would return in the same way (Acts 1:10, 11). Now the early church faced the unknown. Yes, Jesus said He would return, but when?

While exiled on the island of Patmos, John wrote about the promise of Jesus’ second coming: “Behold, He is coming with clouds, and every eye will see Him, even they who pierced Him” (Rev. 1:7). John also received an expansive revelation of the events that would signal the end of time. Besides encouragement and rebuke for the seven churches (Rev. 2, 3), the revelations included visions of judgment (Rev. 14:6–12), of the end of the world (Rev. 20), of the second coming of Christ (Rev. 14:14–16), and of the re-creation of the world (Rev. 21, 22).

John identified himself as a “brother and companion in the tribulation and kingdom and patience of Jesus Christ” (Rev. 1:9). He knew what it was to suffer, not just from the agony of waiting, but from the persecution that came with proclaiming the Word of God and having the testimony of Jesus Christ.

John was invited to see the future and was commanded to write what he saw. In the last chapter, he wrote, “Now I, John, saw and heard these things” (Rev. 22:8). John was a prophetic witness to the events of the last days, commissioned to share with the churches of his day what he had been shown in vision (Rev. 22:16). He’s reminiscent of John the Baptist, who heralded the first advent of Jesus Christ (John 1:6–8) at a crucial moment in earth’s history.

We are called to testify as John did. We too must keep the testimony of Jesus (Rev. 19:10)—the testimony He gave about Himself through the prophets. We may not receive visions as John did, but we can wait patiently for Christ’s return while giving witness to His work in our lives and watching for signs of our deliverance.
