As we journey through this study, it’s important to engage deeply with the material and reflect on its implications for our faith and daily lives. In your Sabbath School class (or Bible study group), use the outline of questions below to lead a thoughtful discussion based on this week’s main passage and supporting verses.

**Messages from heaven:**

1. What steps did God use to convey to the apostle John the message contained in the book of Revelation? Rev. 1:1–3
2. Why was it imperative for some prophets to write down the messages God gave them? Rev. 1:19; Exod. 17:14; 1 Cor. 10:11
3. What are some ways God gives revelations to His prophets? Num. 12:6; Isa. 6:1, 8
4. How does God speak to His people? Heb. 1:1, 2; 2 Pet. 1:19–21

`Personal reflection: Why is it so important that we pay attention when God speaks? See 2 Chron. 36:15, 16.`

**Prophetic writings:**

1. Why are the writings of the prophets needed? 2 Tim. 3:16, 17
2. How did eyewitness accounts and Spirit-inspired historical research contribute to the writings of the Bible? Luke 1:1–3; 1 John 1:1–3
3. What kinds of non-inspired sources did the prophets sometimes quote from? Acts 17:28; Titus 1:12

`Personal reflection: Why is it so important to trust the Bible as God’s inspired Word, even if we do not fully understand how it was written?`

**Other forms of communication:**

1. Did all prophets write books of the Bible? How else did prophets deliver their message besides in writing? 2 Pet. 2:5
2. What kinds of symbols, parables, and illustrations did Bible prophets sometimes use? Ezek. 4:1–8

**Key takeaways to remember:**

- With the Holy Spirit, we have more advantages now than the disciples in Jesus’ physical presence had.
- Pain is not without purpose. Jesus uses our suffering and sorrow to create something beautiful, leading to a joy no one can take away.
- Jesus knew the disciples would abandon Him, but He still assured them that “the Father Himself loves you.” He assures us of the same.
