### Oral Messages

Before anything was recorded in writing, God’s messages came audibly. God spoke to Adam and Eve both before the Fall (Gen. 2:16, 17) and after (Gen. 3:9–19). In both cases, they perfectly understood His words. His voice was heard in clear terms at Sinai (Exod. 20:1–20), at Jesus’ baptism, and at His transfiguration (Matt. 3:17; Matt. 17:5). We may misinterpret what other people say to us, but you can be sure that when God speaks to someone, there is no miscommunication.

The Bible says “God spoke to Moses and said to him: ‘I am the Lord’” (Exod. 6:2). Many Bible writers acknowledged hearing the voice of God as He spoke to them. Such was the case, for example, with Isaiah (Isa. 8:1, 11), Jeremiah (Jer. 1:4, 9, 11), and Ezekiel (Ezek. 3:16; 6:1). Any attempt to deny these and other similar claims ultimately questions the honesty of the prophets and the reliability of the Bible itself.

As God spoke to the prophets in an intelligible human language, so spoke the prophets to their respective audiences. For instance, Noah was a true prophet of God and a faithful “preacher of righteousness” (2 Pet. 2:5). He did not write any portion of the Bible, but he delivered God’s message orally to his contemporaries, and his message was no less inspired than the writings of the canonical prophets. Several other prophets, including Nathan, Elijah, Elisha, and John the Baptist, similarly didn’t write any portion of the Bible, but delivered their messages orally.

And what about Jesus Christ Himself? The Gospel record mentions that during His earthly ministry He “wrote on the ground with His finger” (John 8:6)… but that’s it. We have no record of Jesus writing anything else. But He taught and preached orally “as one having authority” (Matt. 7:29). Some of His listeners even confessed, “No man ever spoke like this Man” (John 7:40, 46). Jesus was primarily an oral communicator of God’s messages. His messages were no less authoritative or binding simply because He did not pen any books of the Bible.

As we’ve seen this week, God sends His message in many different forms. It’s important to remember that we do not choose how God speaks; God chooses how He speaks. We choose whether to listen. At times, we may not want to read. We may wish that God would audibly speak directly to us, but we should humbly and gratefully accept God’s communication in whatever form it comes to us.
