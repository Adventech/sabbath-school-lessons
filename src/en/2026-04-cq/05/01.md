**Read This Week’s Passage:** Revelation 1

### General and Special Revelation

What would the Creator of the cosmos have to say to us? Why does He communicate with us? We’re fallen human beings in a sinful world, and we face death, suffering, and trials. God speaks to us because He wants us to know why we’re here, why things are so bad, and why, despite all the evil around us, we can hope in His love for us.

God often speaks to us nonverbally. His general revelation, as seen in the natural world, speaks eloquently of His infinite power and caring love. King David exclaimed, “The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament shows His handiwork” (Ps. 19:1). The apostle Paul added, “For since the creation of the world His invisible attributes, that is, His eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly perceived, being understood by what has been made, so that they are without excuse” (Rom. 1:20, NASB). God’s restoring power in particular is manifested quite clearly in the mysterious healing processes in our world. Indeed, “God’s healing power runs all through nature. If a tree is cut, if a human being is wounded or breaks a bone, nature begins at once to repair the injury. Even before the need exists, the healing agencies are in readiness; and as soon as a part is wounded, every energy is bent to the work of restoration.”<sup>1</sup>

As beautiful and instructive as the natural world can be, it reveals not only God’s power and love but also the presence of evil (see Gen. 3:14–19; Matt. 13:24–30). This ambiguous message can be properly understood only in light of God’s special revelation—in both Jesus Christ, the incarnate Word of God, and in the Bible, the Written Word of God that testifies of Him (John 5:39). The Scriptures are the inspired record of God’s messages to all human beings throughout the ages. They tell us about the Creation of the world and its tragic fall, God’s amazing plan of redemption, and the final restoration of planet Earth. As brief and selective as some biblical narratives can be, they’re always reliable, because it’s God speaking to us.

Many books and writings, not just the Bible, have come down to us through the ages, yet we deem the Bible the Word of God. What separates it from other writings? Paul wrote, “All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness” (2 Tim. 3:16). There’s some debate regarding the best translation of this verse, but most Bible translations render it in a similar way, emphasizing that the whole Bible is of divine origin (2 Pet. 1:20, 21).

Many people wonder at the inspiration process that led to the Bible. It’s important to recognize that prophetic inspiration is the work of the Holy Spirit, who assisted His prophets in the entire process of communicating His divine messages. He helped the prophets not only receive the messages but also communicate them clearly to their recipients.

God’s messages came to the prophets through a variety of methods—visions and dreams (Num. 12:6; Isa. 6:1, 8), eyewitnesses (1 John 1:1–3), Spirit-inspired historical research (Luke 1:1–3), and divine impressions on the mind (2 Pet. 1:21) are just a few. Once they were received, these messages could be communicated to their original audiences orally (2 Pet. 1:19–21), in written form (John 20:30, 31), or even through dramatization (Ezek. 4:1–8). Regardless of the means the Holy Spirit used, the prophets and biblical authors were given charge of objective—not subjective—messages of truth. It’s a very important point: God’s messages were not to be tampered with or in any way distorted by His messengers, even if they used their own words to express those messages. God carefully protected the messages He gave through the prophets so that we could listen and come to know Him.
