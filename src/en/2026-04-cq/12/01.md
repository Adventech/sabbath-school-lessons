**Read This Week’s Passage:** Acts 2:1–21

### Sunset Provision

In some government legislation, there is a “sunset provision.” This legal term has nothing to do with the setting of the sun. Rather, it’s a clause that automatically expires the statute on a specific date. Governments place such clauses in legislation for a variety of reasons. For instance, they may enact certain laws that abridge citizens’ rights during times of national peril. Governments sometimes include sunset provisions to void these laws on a certain date in order to restore to their citizens the rights that were temporarily taken away.

Did God include a sunset provision when the Holy Spirit first gave the gift of prophecy in ancient times? Is there a time before Jesus’ return when this gift will cease to appear?

To answer these questions, we must carefully consider why the prophetic gift is important for the success of the church. The gift of prophecy is one of many spiritual gifts God has given to help the church flourish. For the church to function well, God has given spiritual gifts to facilitate its ministry. In the New Testament, we find several lists of spiritual gifts, all quite similar, yet different in detail. These gifts complement each other very well:

The purpose of all spiritual gifts is to nurture and build up the church as well as to proclaim the gospel. Each gift is important to develop the church and bring it to full spiritual maturity (Eph. 4:12, 13). Paul included the gift of prophecy (implied from the word “prophets”) in all four lists. Most other gifts occur only once. Looking at the frequency of this inclusion, we can conclude that prophecy is one of the most important spiritual gifts.

Let’s review some general descriptions of how prophets operate in the Bible:

1. Prophets are called by God (see 1 Kings 19:16, 19–21 and Isa. 6). Often, they feel unworthy and are reluctant to assume their responsibility, but they obey.
2. God speaks to prophets by visions, dreams, or audible utterances (Num. 12:6; 24:4). Sometimes God reveals future events. At other times He informs them about the current affairs of nations or people (Dan. 2).
3. The Scriptures describe physical phenomena: prophets may lose physical strength or even stop breathing, but they may also be filled with new power (Dan. 10:8–10, 18, 19).
4. Prophets communicate their messages in various ways—orally (Acts 21:10–12), through dramatizations (Ezek. 4), or by letters (Rev. 2, 3). Certain prophets used literary assistants, as was the case with Baruch, who assisted Jeremiah (Jer. 36:1–4).
5. A prophet’s message must be in agreement with those of former prophets, because God does not contradict Himself (Isa. 8:20).
6. The content of a prophet’s message as well as the way the prophet lives will testify to its divine origin (Matt. 7:15–20).
7. Satan always attempts to counterfeit the divine. This holds true for the prophetic gift as well. In the end times, there will be false prophets (Matt. 24:24; 2 Pet. 2:1).

Understanding how God used prophets in the Bible gives us a proper picture of how God might use prophets today. Last week, we learned that God spoke through prophets to His people at crucial moments. This week, we will examine the prophetic gift at the end of time—its special function and perpetuity—as well as the role of Ellen G. White’s prophetic ministry as the second coming of Jesus draws closer.
