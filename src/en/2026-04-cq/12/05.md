### The Testimony of Jesus

Revelation 12 gives a brief history of the great controversy, from its inception in heaven until the last days. In Revelation 12:17, two identifying characteristics of the end-time church are mentioned: it holds to and keeps the Ten Commandments (which includes the Sabbath commandment) and has the “testimony of Jesus.” What does the “testimony of Jesus” mean?

The original Greek text can be rendered in two different ways. On the one hand, it can be understood as “the testimony about Jesus,” in the sense of believers witnessing about Him, making Him known through our mission and evangelism (see Rev. 19:10, NIV: “hold to the testimony of Jesus”). On the other hand, it can be understood as “the testimony from Jesus,” meaning Jesus revealing truth to His church (Rev. 19:10, GNT: “the truth that Jesus revealed”). Since both are valid translations, is there a way to determine what John meant?

If we compare the two identifying marks in Revelation 12:17, we see a strong similarity in the way they’re worded (the parallel phrasing is even more evident in the Greek). Just as the phrase “commandments of God” naturally means commandments that come from God, so the parallel phrase “testimony of Jesus” can be understood as a testimony that comes from Jesus. Considering this parallel, the “testimony of Jesus” would be something that Jesus gives.

The “testimony of Jesus” is clearly identified with the “spirit of prophecy” in Revelation 19:10 and Revelation 22:9. This makes sense, because if the testimony comes from Jesus, it may refer to prophetic revelation given by Him to His church. A prophet is a person with a special message from God to His people, whether to exhort and call to repentance, to encourage, or to reveal the future. John himself was a prophet, and his ministry is an example of what it means to bear the testimony of Jesus.

Revelation 12:17 shows that Jesus makes His church recognizable through this gift of prophecy. This means that just as God sent prophets to the Old Testament people of Israel and Judah and the apostle John to the early Christian church, prophets will also arise in the last days of earth’s history.

The Bible does not leave believers uninformed about whether God will send prophets in the last days. People who study the Bible should anticipate and look for the modern manifestation of the prophetic gift. God’s end-time people can be confident that He will send prophets with important messages to help them.
