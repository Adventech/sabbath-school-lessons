**Read This Week’s Passage:** 2 Chronicles 20

### Prophecy Affirms the Bible

God never asks us to believe blindly. On the contrary, the evidence for His existence is powerful. Nature alone, even after six thousand years of sin, testifies to the reality of not just a God of incredible creative power (look at photos from the James Webb Space Telescope) but also a God of love. The evidence pointing to God is not limited to the wonders of nature. Peter experienced the kind of event so many people desire when he heard the “voice which came from heaven when we were with Him on the holy mountain.” And yet Peter believed there was greater evidence than being an eyewitness. He wrote, “We have the prophetic word confirmed, which you do well to heed as a light that shines in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts” (2 Pet. 1:18, 19). We might not hear the literal voice of God like Peter did, but we too have the “prophetic word confirmed.”

God has spoken to us through His prophets, both canonical (writings in the Bible) and non-canonical (writings not in the Bible). The evidence we’ve been given is more than abundant; the question remaining is whether we’re open to receive it. Sacred history shows that many were not open. May we, by God’s grace, not make the same mistake.

God longs to give good gifts to His people. He favors His church with the spiritual resources needed to accomplish its mission. He shows His special care for His people by sending prophets to counsel and instruct them. Moreover, He blesses those who listen and follow. The ministry of prophets helps the church in many ways.

Prophets revive the church spiritually by calling people back to the basics of Christianity, pointing people to Scripture as the basis of their faith. The apostle Paul is an example of a prophet who stressed the importance of anchoring all our beliefs in the Bible (2 Tim. 3:16). Paul warned young Timothy that many believers would be deceived and drawn away from the teachings of Scripture by false teachers (2 Tim. 3:13). He predicted false teachers would become more and more prevalent until the end of time, meaning we will be increasingly reliant on Scripture to discern the difference between true and false doctrines.

When Paul called people back to Scripture, he followed the example of Jesus, the greatest Prophet ever. Jesus, the One who alone is the Word (John 1:1–3), exalted Scripture (John 7:38). After His resurrection, He used the Scriptures to strengthen the faith of doubting disciples, even before revealing Himself as the risen Christ (Luke 24:27). Among the most precious blessings of the gift of prophecy is that it affirms the Bible.

“Those who have been impressed by the Holy Scriptures as the voice of God, and desire to follow its teachings, are to be daily learning, daily receiving spiritual fervor and power, which have been provided for every true believer in the gift of the Holy Spirit.”<sup>1</sup> The authentic gift of prophecy always exalts Scripture. It always points people back to the Word of God as their only rule of faith and practice.

Without the Bible, human beings would be left without a way to navigate the falsities and temptations of a sinful world, and that would be a recipe for utter destruction. Today, the Bible is everywhere and is available in multiple formats, but this was not always the case. For instance, during Jesus’ and Paul’s time, only the Old Testament scrolls were available to be read, and only priests or rabbis could do so. Common people usually had no direct access to Scripture. A word from God was precious in those days.

Many modern teachers who claim to be prophets subtly undermine the Bible and lead people away from its truths. Genuine prophets, by contrast, strengthen people’s faith in the Bible.

Besides deepening people’s faith and understanding of the Scriptures, prophets inspire and challenge the church to grow and be more faithful to its mission. This last lesson considers how the prophetic word blesses those who pay attention.
