### Prophecy is God’s Truth Detector

Is there still a blessing for those who believe the prophets today? The modern Christian world is complex. Some scholars estimate that there are more than 45,000 Christian denominations in the world today. How could a faith that started with such single-minded unity under Jesus Christ have split into so many different belief systems? The answer lies in the many different interpretations of Scripture that have developed over time. Obviously, they can’t all be correct—in many cases, what one church teaches directly contradicts what others teach.

From the beginning (see Gen. 2:16, 17; 3:1–5), confusion has been one of Satan’s greatest ways of leading humans away from God’s truth. As he did with Adam and Eve, Satan mixes just enough truth with error to pull men and women under His control. Satan has misled God’s people through all time (2 Chron. 36:14–16). He even attempted to mislead Jesus (Matt. 4:1–11), but Jesus met Satan’s deceptions with a clear “It is written” (Matt. 4:4).

Paul foresaw a time when Satan’s deceptions would become increasingly refined (2 Tim. 4:1–5). True teachers would be distinguished by faithfully preaching the Word, no matter how unpopular. People who resist the truth would go in search of spiritual teachers who would excite them with cunningly devised fables. Such people would be overcome by overwhelming delusions: “And for this reason God will send them strong delusion, that they should believe the lie, that they all may be condemned who did not believe the truth but had pleasure in unrighteousness” (2 Thess. 2:11, 12). Paul predicted that the religious world would become a confusing place full of contradictory doctrinal teachings, which is exactly what we see today.

But to those who were open, Jesus promised that they would “know the truth” (John 8:32). The prophetic writings preserved in Scripture is God’s means of separating truth from error, light from darkness. The teachings of the Bible are not taught equally in every Christian church.

If you’re going to insist on keeping the seventh-day Sabbath, which is firmly biblical, you’ve pretty much disqualified 99.5 percent of your options, as the vast majority of churches do not keep the fourth commandment. If you’re also not going to believe that the dead either go immediately into heaven to be with Jesus (not biblical) or are currently burning in hell and will for all eternity (also not biblical)… well, you’ve eliminated 99.5 percent of the 0.5 percent left. If those two doctrines are important to you, the Seventh-day Adventist Church is one of your only options.
