### Prophecy Exalts Christ

Prophecy is one of God’s means of communicating with His people and connecting them to Him. No wonder, then, that Revelation 12:17 says “the dragon [Satan] was enraged with the woman [God’s church] and went to make war with the remnant of her seed” (KJV). What are the distinguishing marks of this remnant of the woman’s seed? They keep God’s commandments and possess the testimony of Jesus. Revelation 19:10 tells us the testimony of Jesus is “the spirit of prophecy.” Jesus is the central theme of this end-time prophetic message.

An end-time prophetic message centered on Christ mirrors the gift of prophecy given to all the New Testament apostles. Jesus was the substance of their messages. Paul wrote to the Corinthians, “For I determined not to know anything among you except Jesus Christ and Him crucified” (1 Cor. 2:2). To the Galatian believers, he wrote, “God forbid that I should boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ” (Gal. 6:14). True prophecy always calls people to a deeper understanding of Jesus and a stronger commitment to Him.

Besides their personal experiences with Him, the apostles accepted Jesus based on the testimony of prophets and righteous men, whose words stretched over a period of many centuries. The Christian world has a full and complete chain of evidence running through both the Old and New Testaments: the former points to a Savior to come, and the latter reveals Jesus as fulfilling the Old Testament prophecies of the Savior. This is enough evidence to establish the faith of those willing to believe. “The design of God was to leave the race a fair opportunity to develop faith in the power of God and of his Son and in the work of the Holy Spirit.”<sup>1</sup>

In many ways, the issue of saving faith is not intellectual, it’s moral. It’s a heart thing, not a head thing. The testimony of Jesus is a heart call to follow Him completely. When people close their hearts, their minds unfortunately follow.

In his Gospel, John introduced Jesus as the eternal Word that created all things (John 1:1–5). If Jesus, the Word of God, existed before Creation, was made flesh, and dwelled among us, it follows that Jesus was integral to the messages God gave to His prophets. Even prophets who bore messages that did not explicitly refer to Jesus were inspired by the Holy Spirit.

God has magnified Jesus through His prophets’ messages. Through the prophetic messages in Scripture and those that have come since, Jesus invites His people to know Him and follow Him. The question is whether we will accept His call.

**Questions to consider:**

`Consider how vast the cosmos is, and marvel that the One who personally created it, Jesus, is also the One who died for you. What great hope does that offer you?`
