As we journey through this study, it’s important to engage deeply with the material and reflect on its implications for our faith and daily lives. In your Sabbath School class (or Bible study group), use the outline of questions below to lead a thoughtful discussion based on this week’s main passage and supporting verses.

**Communication in Eden:**

1. What kind of communication did God originally intend to have with us? Gen. 1:26; 2:18, 19
2. How did the serpent (Satan) undermine trust in God’s communication with our first parents? Gen. 2:16, 17; 3:1–6
3. How did God take the initiative to reestablish communication after Adam’s and Eve’s devastating decision? Gen. 3:8–13
4. How did Adam and Eve try to avoid God and avoid responsibility?

`Personal reflection: In your own life, how has God come seeking after you?`

**God still speaks:**

1. How does God speak through nature? Ps. 19:1–4; Rom. 1:19, 20
2. How has God spoken through prophets and Scripture? Num. 12:6; 2 Tim. 3:16, 17
3. What’s the most important way God has spoken on earth? John 14:7–9; Heb. 1:1, 2
4. After Jesus returned to heaven, who came to teach and guide us? John 16:13

`Personal reflection: How can we get better at hearing God’s voice and discerning the Holy Spirit’s guidance?`

**Key takeaways to remember:**

- Humans were created to enjoy face-to-face communion with God and one another.
- Sin disrupted open communication with God, but God initiated a plan to heal and restore.
- God speaks to people through nature, by sending messages through prophets, and most importantly, by sending Jesus into this world.
