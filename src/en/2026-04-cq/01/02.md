### Hiding from God

Because God is love (1 John 4:8), He would never create human “robots.” Love embraces choice. To be able to love, Adam and Eve had to be created with free will. Otherwise, love—true love, would’ve been impossible for them to give. Thus Adam and Eve had the freedom to obey or disobey their Creator. The reality of this free choice was displayed in the command not to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (see Gen. 2:15–17). Why warn them against eating from it unless they had the freedom to do so in the first place?

Our first parents, though created innocent and holy, were not placed beyond the possibility of wrongdoing. God made them free moral agents, capable of appreciating the wisdom and benevolence of His character and the justice of His requirements, and with full liberty to yield or to withhold obedience. They were to enjoy communion with God and with holy angels. . . . Without freedom of choice, his obedience would not have been voluntary, but forced.<sup>1</sup>

Genesis 3 depicts an awful scene: the fall of Adam and Eve. After the conversation between the serpent (Satan) and Eve, our first parents were expressly tempted to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Gen. 2:17). Satan used the power of words, emotions, and pleasurable enticements to convince Eve, who then convinced Adam, to go against God’s command. Eve noted that the tree was “good for food,” “pleasant to the eyes,” and “desirable to make one wise” (Gen. 3:6). Satan’s promise that they would be “like God, knowing good and evil” (Gen. 3:5) sounded too good to pass up.

In reality, Satan’s discourse was riddled with errors. Satan also hid from them the consequences of their disobedience. After they disobeyed God, their eyes were immediately “opened,” and they saw that they were “naked” (Gen. 3:7). God’s “garment” of light and glory (Ps. 104:1, 2), which reflected the divine image, was gone. They were no longer covered by that which God had provided for them. Adam and Eve attempted to hide themselves from their Creator (as if it’s possible to hide from God), because sin makes us feel guilty and ashamed, and in our guilt, we want to flee from the presence of God.

**Questions to explore:**

`What have you felt after doing something that wasn’t right? What were the consequences? How did your faith in God, and the promise of the gospel, keep you from giving up on faith entirely?`

`Why did Adam and Eve attempt to hide from their Creator after they disobeyed Him? Compare Gen. 3:1–8 with Jer. 23:24 and Heb. 4:13.`
