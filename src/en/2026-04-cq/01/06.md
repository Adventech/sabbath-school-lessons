### Ellen G. White

God’s communication is not limited to biblical times. As we will see in the coming weeks, God speaks to human beings in perpetuity, and His words will be manifested in modern ways until Christ returns.

Ellen Gould Harmon White and her twin sister Elizabeth were born on November 26, 1827, in Gorham, Maine. They were the youngest of eight children born to Eunice and Robert Harmon. A few years later, the family moved to the city of Portland, Maine, where Robert hoped to expand his hat-making business. At age nine, while in a park near her home, an angry classmate threw a stone that struck Ellen in the face, leaving her in a semi-conscious state for several weeks. This accident abruptly ended her formal education and left her with lifelong health challenges. The facial injuries also left Ellen self-conscious about her appearance, limiting her social interactions.

In this context of uncertainty and struggle, the young Ellen Harmon began to think about her spiritual destiny. She was afraid of God and felt unworthy of heaven. Such fearful feelings intensified when the Harmon family began preparing for the Second Coming in accordance with Millerite teaching of Christ’s soon return. Even after her baptism by immersion into the Methodist Church in 1842, Ellen did not feel worthy of God’s love. She believed she was “forever lost.”<sup>1</sup>

Amid these agonizing thoughts, young Ellen had two dreams that began to change her mind about God. However, it was not until her mid-teens when she met Levi Stockman, a Methodist preacher, that she understood and felt God’s love and grace. It was as if God spoke directly to her in her despair. “Faith now took possession of my heart,” she wrote later. “I felt an inexpressible love for God, and had the witness of His Spirit that my sins were pardoned. My views of the Father were changed. I now looked upon Him as a kind and tender parent, rather than a stern tyrant compelling men to a blind obedience. My heart went out toward Him in a deep and fervent love.”<sup>2</sup> At the same time, Ellen also understood the doctrine of the “state of the dead” and gradually grasped the idea that there was no forever-burning hell. Her new understanding of God and His love completely changed her mindset. As we will learn each Friday in the weeks to come, Ellen fell deeply in love with Jesus and served Him for the rest of her life.
