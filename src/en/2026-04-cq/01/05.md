### God Still Speaks

Despite sin, God found many ways to communicate with fallen human beings. One way God reveals Himself to us is through creation. Nature is a powerful revelation of His wonderful acts. Scientists are amazed by the wonders of our universe and at the complexity of even the “simplest” life. Yet nature also displays the consequences of the Fall, with the death of every living thing the most powerful example.

Another way God has spoken to humanity is through the prophets—individuals whom He chose as His spokespersons. For example, God spoke to Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Daniel, revealing His will for His people. At their core, God’s prophetic messages are a reminder that He has not forgotten His children and is still with them.

Some prophets, such as Enoch, Nathan, Huldah, and John the Baptist, didn’t write any books of the Bible. Their messages were either intended for a certain time and place or were communicated by other Bible writers. However, the writings of many prophets are preserved in Scripture for our benefit (2 Tim. 3:16, 17). The Bible is a revelation of God Himself. It’s through His words that we learn who He is, see His relentless love, and discover His instructions for how we’re to live. Moreover, it’s through the Bible that we understand who we are, where we came from, and where we’re going. There we find God’s purpose for our lives.

The most effective way God has spoken to us is through Jesus Christ, who, of course, is revealed to us in the Bible. As God sought Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden, so Christ came to “seek and to save that which was lost” (Luke 19:10). Jesus declared, “He who has seen Me has seen the Father” (John 14:9). Through His life, death, and resurrection, Christ fulfilled the promise given to Adam and Eve in Eden (Gen. 3:15). He came and died in our place so that we can enjoy both eternal life and restored communion with God.

Since Jesus’ ascension to heaven, the Holy Spirit has continued to guide His people “into all the truth” and to speak to us of “what is yet to come” (John 16:13, NIV). One way He guides people into truth is by giving certain individuals messages from God for His people (2 Pet. 1:21), such as when He inspired the apostles with prophetic messages that were then written down and preserved in the books of the New Testament.
