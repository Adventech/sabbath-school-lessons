### God Seeks Humanity

We can see from Genesis 3:9, 10 that God took the initiative to seek His fallen children. The question “Where are you?” was rhetorical, because God knew what Adam and Eve had done and where they were. Sin had cut off the usual communication that existed between Him and the first human couple. God’s immediate concern was to restore communication with His children, to meet with them, and to give them hope in their despair. In fact, the Hebrew verb qara’, meaning “call,” in Genesis 3:9 is often used to describe God’s call to establish communication with His people.

God’s seeking Adam and Eve reveals His unconditional love for His children. God never gives up on us. He goes to great lengths to reestablish communion with us, even when we go into hiding. Before Jesus’ crucifixion, when Peter denied Jesus three times, Christ “turned and looked” (Luke 22:61) at him. Even without words, He sought to reach and reconnect with His erring friend.

Notice in Genesis 3:11–13 how sin suddenly changed Adam’s and Eve’s relationships both with God and each other. Instead of confessing their sin, they began to blame others. Adam blamed Eve, thus indirectly blaming God because God had given Eve to him: “The woman whom You gave to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I ate” (Gen. 3:12). Eve, meanwhile, blamed the serpent for her disobedience: “The serpent deceived me, and I ate” (Gen. 3:13). Neither of them, at least when first confronted, owned up to their own guilt. How little we’ve changed in six thousand years.

The reality is that sin has affected all human beings. The apostle Paul described our condition well when he detailed the pain of desiring to do what’s right, only to find himself doing the opposite: “For what I am doing, I do not understand. For what I will to do, that I do not practice; but what I hate, that I do” (Rom. 7:15).

Who can’t relate? We are all broken people who keep doing things we know we should not do. Who hasn’t experienced this reality?

Our only hope is deliverance “through Jesus Christ our Lord” (Rom. 7:25). This fact is why God continues to seek us. He wants desperately to lead us to Jesus, who alone can mold us into His perfect image and save us from the devastation caused by sin.

God never gives up on us. He goes to great lengths to reestablish communion with us, even when we go into hiding.
