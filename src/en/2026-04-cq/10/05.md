### Jesus’ Divine Authority

While Jesus was teaching, the priests came to Him and demanded to know who had given Him the authority to do His ministry (Matt. 21:23–27). After all, Jesus had not graduated from any of the local schools, nor had He received training in the traditional schools of the rabbis. Rather than answering their questions, Jesus pointed the way to the answer, asking His interrogators a very clever question about the unmistakable authority exhibited by John the Baptist. By refusing to answer, they negated the power of God that crowned John’s ministry and rejected the authority of Jesus who, like John, was also sent by God.

All authority belongs to Jesus as humanity’s Creator (John 1:3) and Savior (Rom. 3:24). He is the living Word of God. The divine authority that Jesus possessed during His earthly life and ministry could not be imitated or self-generated by anyone. Jesus’ teaching astonished His listeners because He taught as One having authority (Matt. 7:29).

All the Gospels record Jesus’ divine authority. Jesus forgave sins (Mark 2:10, 11), drove out demons (Matt. 17:18), and perceived people’s thoughts (John 2:24, 25). At His command, the sea became calm, and the storm ceased (Luke 8:22–25). He raised the dead and offered eternal life (John 10:28). He claimed authority to exercise judgment over people’s eternal destinies (John 5:25–29). While on earth as our Redeemer, Jesus exercised the authority given to Him by God the Father (John 17:2), and He depended on and cooperated with the Father (John 5:19) at all times. He often referred back to the Scriptures, which He claimed testified about Him (John 5:39). Jesus also possessed absolute authority and could delegate that authority to His disciples (Mark 6:7). As the Word of God, His testimony also came to the prophets through whom He spoke (Rev. 19:10).

Christ’s authority is complete, universal, and eternal. He declares, “All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth” (Matt. 28:18). Christ received His authority from the Father. He who rejects Christ rejects the Father.

Like the religious leaders in Jesus’ time, we each have the opportunity to make our own decision regarding whether we personally accept Christ’s authority. Our openness to Christ is often revealed in the way we receive those He sends to us. Jesus told His disciples, “He who hears you hears Me, he who rejects you rejects Me, and he who rejects Me rejects Him who sent Me” (Luke 10:16). May we embrace Christ, His message, and His messengers.
