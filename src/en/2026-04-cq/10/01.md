**Read This Week’s Passage:** Exodus 3, 4

### Delegated Authority

On November 9, 1989, Günter Schabowski’s statement on live TV in Communist East Germany set in motion the fall of the Berlin Wall. Speaking as East Germany’s press spokesperson, Schabowski botched a statement during the televised press conference. Instead of announcing that East Germans may be allowed to visit Western Europe after the lengthy and difficult process of obtaining the proper visas, he simply said East Germans would be permitted to visit Western Europe. When a reporter asked when the policy change would take effect, Schabowski said, “As far as I know, this enters into force . . . immediately, without delay.” Within minutes people began pouring toward border checkpoints, demanding to be let through as the government had announced.

People have often been given authority to speak for a company or even a country. But what about those who speak for God? What authority do biblical prophets possess?

The Bible is God’s unique means of revealing Himself to humanity. Through Scripture, we learn why we’re here, God’s character and will, and God’s purpose for our lives. The Bible is the gold standard of truth, the authority by which we must make life choices. It’s the foundation of the Christian worldview and the basis of all revealed truth. It’s the Christian’s rule of faith and practice.

We must either accept or reject God’s Word. Rejecting the Word need not be as extreme as burning our Bibles, as King Jehoiakim did with Jeremiah’s prophetic message (Jer. 36:22–27). Merely ignoring it while professing to believe it can be just as problematic. However, when we discover and accept God’s Word, we can, like King Josiah, be transformed and lead others to God (see 2 Kings 22:10–13).

People often approach Scripture to look for its faults or ridicule it. Some see the Bible as out of step with contemporary trends (it usually is, and if we’re honest, it should be). Many deride it because it contradicts “scientific” or cultural beliefs and trends.

In Christianity, the Bible is often used only to support a particular theological position rather than as a means for personal spiritual growth and reformation. Others interpret the Bible in a way that denies any historical value or practical authority it has over our daily lives.

One of the easiest and perhaps most common ways of rejecting biblical authority is to ignore the Bible and not take the time to read and then apply Scripture to our lives. The Word calls us to repentance and obedience (Acts 5:29; see also Heb. 4:12). That’s often why people reject it. They should (and sometimes do) know better, but they don’t like its claims on their lives.

Another important issue to consider is the authority of non-canonical prophets—those whose writings were not included in the Bible, such as Nathan, John the Baptist, and even Ellen G. White. What, if any, authority do they hold?

After King David committed adultery and murder, God sent the prophet Nathan to confront him (2 Sam. 12:7–14). Although Nathan had not written a biblical book, David did not question Nathan’s authority, and that’s because the authority of the prophets’ message is based on their inspiration, not on whether they were included in the biblical canon. Nevertheless, all true non-canonical prophets—_especially_ a modern prophet such as Ellen White—saw themselves as subject to biblical authority; they did not hold themselves above the Bible. Their messages must be tested by the Bible and will never replace the Bible as a source of faith and doctrine.

Though we don’t know why God permitted some prophets’ messages to be included in the Bible while others were omitted, we do know the non-canonical prophets were equally inspired by God. The Bible teaches that spiritual gifts, including the gift of prophecy, will continue until the end of time. Prophets are still needed today “for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ, till we all come to the unity of the faith” (Eph. 4:12, 13). For that reason, God promised to send visions and dreams in the last days to guide His people (Joel 2:28–31). Some people claim that God never called any more prophets after the time of the Bible. However, it’s because of the Bible that we can expect prophets in the last days.
