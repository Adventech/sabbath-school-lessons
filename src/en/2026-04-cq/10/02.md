### Mediating God’s Authority

Before giving a person permission to speak for you, wouldn’t you make sure you’ve chosen someone you can trust? It would be vital that he or she knew your position and beliefs and faithfully expressed them.

When he was forty years old, Moses made a grand attempt to deliver the children of Israel from their cruel bondage, but his effort backfired terribly, and he had to flee Egypt to save his own life (Exod. 2:11–15). Moses had spent the first forty years of his life getting the best education that Egypt had to offer, but it did not prepare him to become God’s messenger. He had to spend the next forty years in the wilderness as a shepherd, without the distractions of the world. In many ways, he had to unlearn much of what he had learned as a prince. Before he could cooperate in God’s plan to free Israel, Moses had to get to know the One on whose behalf he would be speaking.

Sometimes we need to experience the silence of the wilderness before we’re ready to listen to God. This was certainly true for Moses. When Moses was eighty years old, God appeared to him and asked him to be His mouthpiece. Moses felt reluctant, insecure, and unqualified (Exod. 3:11). Hearing his hesitation, God promised to be with him and give him success (Exod. 3:12).

Despite God’s promises, Moses doubted that anyone would listen to him (Exod. 4:1). He knew he had previously failed. He remembered his last encounter with his fellow Israelites, when his right to lead had been questioned (Exod. 2:14). God understood Moses’ apprehension. He dealt patiently with his fears. All grounds for excuse had been removed, but even then, Moses resisted the call. He insisted it would be better for God to send someone else (Exod. 4:13). His fears had become unbelief. By his persistent reluctance, he questioned God’s ability to use him. It was then that God spoke more firmly, rebuking the reluctant prophet to get him moving (Exod. 4:14–17).

Moses was asked to play the role of God, with Aaron assuming the role of Moses’ mouthpiece (Exod. 4:16). As a spokesperson for Moses, Aaron was a type of “prophet” who delivered messages on his brother’s behalf (Exod. 7:1, 2). As Moses gave Aaron messages for Pharoah, so God gives His prophets messages for His people. Just as He did with Moses and Aaron, so God helps His prophets find the best way to deliver the messages He gives them.

**Questions to explore:**

`You may not be a prophet, but what has God called you to do for Him during your lifetime? What’s the best way to prepare yourself for this calling? If you don’t know, where or to whom can you go for guidance?`

`If we love God, why is it important to carefully consider how we receive His messengers?`
