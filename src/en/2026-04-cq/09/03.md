### Conditional Prophecies

People often think of a prophet as someone who predicts the future, but that is only one aspect of a prophet’s work. Prophets lead people back to God’s Word and provide counsel, warning, and encouragement.

Besides helping people know about and prepare for future events, prophecies help people know which prophets can be trusted. The predictions of a true prophet should always happen as prophesied: “When a prophet speaks in the name of the Lord, if the thing does not happen or come to pass, that is the thing which the Lord has not spoken; the prophet has spoken it presumptuously; you shall not be afraid of him” (Deut. 18:22). God’s people should understand that a true prophet’s predictions are reliable.

Many prophets delivered competing messages in Jeremiah’s days. Most of them promised a bright future for Jerusalem, but Jeremiah predicted disaster for the city. He challenged people to compare his predictions with those of other prophets of his time and see whose prophecies were true (Jer. 28:9, 15–17). The people could know which prophet was trustworthy by identifying whose predictions were accurate.

While helpful, this test can be challenging to apply because some prophecies are conditional—they might not come to pass as the prophet predicted if the conditions were not met. We see this idea with Jonah and his warning against Nineveh. Jonah’s prediction was fairly short and pointed: “Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!” (Jon. 3:4). Even though he was sent by God (and was therefore a true prophet), it didn’t happen as he predicted. Why?

Conditional prophecies depend on how people respond to God’s messages. God promised that even after He announces judgment against a wicked nation, He will reverse the approaching judgment if that nation repents of its sins (Jer. 18:7, 8). Similarly, when God promises to prosper a nation, that prophecy is given on condition of their faithfulness to Him. God will withdraw His promised blessings from the nation that turns away from Him (Jer. 18:9, 10). If the people change their attitudes and behavior, the predicted event may not happen. The people of Nineveh took Jonah’s prediction to heart and changed their ways, so God did not destroy them (Jon. 3:10). When later generations forgot this prophetic warning and Nineveh again became an evil, cruel place, God destroyed it. Our choices greatly determine our future realities.
