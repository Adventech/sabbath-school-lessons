### “Taste and See”

From the inception of the church, Ellen G. White has faced similar challenges to those of many biblical prophets. Some questioned her prophetic claims after her very first vision in December 1844. This skepticism and distrust led Sabbath-keeping Adventists (later called Seventh-day Adventists) to develop arguments in support of Ellen White’s prophetic gift.

First, they addressed whether the gift of prophecy ended with Jesus, offering theological justification for their belief that it will continue until the end of time. Based on Joel 2:28, 29, Revelation 12:17, and other scriptures, they argued that the prophetic gift would be visible in the “last days” and would become an “identifying mark” of God’s end-time people.<sup>1</sup>

Second, early Adventists noted that Ellen White’s gift had a positive influence on believers. For example, her prophetic insights helped to unify believers attending the late 1840s Sabbath Conferences when they disagreed on biblical doctrines. Her gift continued to bear “good fruit” in the decades that followed, and her writings remain influential within the Seventh-day Adventist community to this day.

A third argument relates to the supernatural elements of her visionary experiences. As we have noted previously, while in vision, Ellen White exhibited supernatural manifestations—reporting facts and circumstances unknown to her, revealing future events, exhibiting greater-than-natural strength, not breathing while in vision, and being unable to control or influence the timing of her visionary experiences, among other phenomena.

Fourth, in harmony with 1 John 4, Ellen White’s prophetic messages consistently directed people to the Scriptures and uplifted Christ. She wrote: “Lift up Jesus. . . . Let all your powers be directed to pointing souls, confused, bewildered, lost, to ‘the Lamb of God.’”<sup>2</sup> At her last address to a General Conference Session (a business meeting where leaders from the world church assemble), she held up the Bible and said, “Brethren and Sisters, I commend unto you this Book.”<sup>3</sup>

Seventh-day Adventists today continue to highlight these evidences for the validity of Ellen White’s gift. While these arguments are sound, a powerful “proof” of her gift is a personal experience with her writings. As the psalmist David urged: “Taste and see that the Lord is good” (Ps. 34:8). It’s only when we prayerfully read Ellen White’s writings that we can evaluate her prophetic claims.

One place to begin is with her small but most popular volume, Steps to Christ. Follow this with the five-volume Conflict of the Ages series, which traces the great controversy theme from Genesis to Revelation. Her writings are widely available in paper and electronic versions (see EGWWritings.org) and have been translated into many languages. Test her for yourself.
