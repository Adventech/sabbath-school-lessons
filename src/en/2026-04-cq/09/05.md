### The Incarnation of Christ

What a prophet teaches about Jesus is another key test. In accordance with 1 John 4:1–3, exploring whether a prophet believes in Jesus’ incarnation quickly rules out people who claim to be prophets from other major world religions, or from those who claim to connect to the spiritual realm without subscribing to any particular religion. If a person “does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh,” that person “is not of God” (1 John 4:3).

When John wrote these verses, he was thinking of false teachers who were teaching that Jesus was not really a human being and only appeared to be human. This test, however, goes further. It isn’t saying only that a prophet should agree that Jesus was a real person who lived on earth. Even many secular historians believe this about Jesus. John was dealing with a very specific issue at the time—but his prophetic words contain important truths with a wider application for later generations as well.

True prophets believe that Jesus was God incarnate, yet also fully a human being (John 1:1–3, 14). They teach that Jesus was tempted and yet had no sin (Heb. 4:15; 2 Cor. 5:21). Their ministry helps people understand Jesus’ mission to save sinners (John 3:16). They build people’s faith in the resurrection and help people look forward to His second coming (John 11:25; 14:1–3). They embrace the complete truth about Jesus—His mission, sinless life, atoning death, resurrection, high-priestly ministry in heaven, and Second Coming. It must be more than an intellectual agreement that Jesus lived on earth. Christ must be the center of a true prophet’s teachings and not just something added on. His or her messages should lead people to a saving relationship with Jesus.

The Spirit-inspired prophetic word is God’s spotlight, dispelling the darkness in us until Jesus, the Morning Star, dawns in our hearts, and the prophetic gift unites us with Him and continually points us to Him.

Prophets who uplift Jesus do not promote themselves or try to create their own following (Acts 4:12). They’re ready to serve and don’t take advantage of those to whom they’ve been sent. At the same time, God’s prophets are human beings and, like the rest of us, sinners who need the gospel.

**Questions to consider:**

`Why is understanding that Jesus, who is fully God, died as our substitute and bore the penalty for our sins so central to our faith? What do we lose if we lose that teaching?`
