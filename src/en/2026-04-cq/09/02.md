### Positive Fruits

In the Sermon on the Mount (Matt. 5–7), Jesus outlined the foundations of the Christian church, which included instruction for testing prophets. Jesus warned that history would be full of false prophets (Matt. 7:15) and gave believers tools to detect the difference between the true and the false: “By their fruits you will know them” (Matt. 7:20).

The quality of a fruit can be judged only over a period of time. A tree does not bear fruit instantly. However, over time, a tree becomes known by its fruit. A tree that bears bad fruit or no fruit cannot hide the problem forever. Eventually, the fruit reveals the truth about the tree. Many false prophets have been exposed for leading people into sin. People claiming to be prophets sometimes speak the most arrogantly, live the most extravagantly, and lead the most shocking double lives. By seeing a prophet’s character and watching what results come from his or her messages, we can see whether that prophet is drawing others to God or leading them away from Him. In their words and lifestyle, a true prophet reflects the character of Jesus.

Being a prophet, however, does not make a person sinless or infallible. We see mistakes or character flaws in the biblical examples of Jonah, Paul, and Peter (Jon. 1:1–3; Acts 15:36–39; Gal. 2:11–14). God alone is flawless; His messengers never are. Prophets, like all of us, have weaknesses, but the trend of their lives must clearly be toward God, and even if they speak truth, the prophets must not negate their ministry by a lifestyle that is contrary to the message they share.

No prophet perfectly imitates the example of Jesus, but a true prophet is committed to the principles and lifestyle of God’s kingdom (Matt. 7:16–20). Anyone who claims to be God’s messenger yet whose lifestyle blatantly contradicts the message he or she carries should be regarded with suspicion.

Although the prophetic call is difficult, many prophets showed good fruit during their ministries. For example, Moses successfully led the children of Israel through the wilderness (Deut. 34:10–12). Haggai’s faithfulness encouraged the inhabitants of Judah to start rebuilding the second temple, which had been put on pause for nineteen years (Hag. 1:12). Isaiah’s unflinching ministry served four Judean kings, guiding them through a tumultuous period and pointing indisputably to the Messiah (Isa. 1:1; 39:5, 6; 53:4–6). We cannot forget Daniel’s heartfelt confession (Dan. 9:15) or Jeremiah’s tear-filled lament (Jer. 9:1), both made on the people’s behalf. Notwithstanding how they often were persecuted and mistreated, God’s prophets always showed a genuine love for Him and His people, which is a hallmark of a fruitful ministry.

**Questions to explore:**

`What kind of fruit might you find in the life of someone who claimed to be a prophet?`

`How often have men or women of God disappointed you by their actions or character flaws? What important lessons can you learn from those experiences, remembering that God alone is perfect?`
