### The Gift of Prophecy

Neuroscientists have begun to understand human infants’ incredible ability to learn language. At six months of age, a baby can understand the sounds that comprise any of the seven thousand-plus spoken languages in the world. If exposed to a second language, they can acquire that language as well. During this period, a mysterious mental door opens, allowing infants to distinguish the forty or so sounds that generally make up a native language from the eight hundred different sounds that they can perceive at birth. But there is one catch: babies must be spoken to. You might call it voice lessons for newborns.

Why would God create us with this wonderful capacity for speech recognition and language acquisition if He never intended to speak to us? The question is rhetorical, of course; the Bible declares, “God, who at various times and in various ways spoke in time past to the fathers by the prophets, has in these last days spoken to us by His Son, whom He has appointed heir of all things, through whom also He made the worlds” (Heb. 1:1, 2).

When sin shattered Adam’s and Eve’s communion with God, He did not wash His hands of the fallen pair and leave them to self-destruct. He came looking for them both, asking Adam, “Where are you?” (Gen. 3:9). He went on to prophesy about the birth of One who would save the world from sin (Gen. 3:15). From the beginning, right there in Eden, right after the Fall, prophecy uplifted Jesus and pointed sinners to Him, our only hope.

This quarter we will study the gift of prophecy, one of God’s ingenious methods of communicating His will to fallen human beings, who can no longer commune face-to-face with Him. This special gift of the Holy Spirit is mentioned prominently in 1 Corinthians 12, 1 Corinthians 14, Ephesians 4, and Romans 12. The Holy Spirit is the Great Dispenser of this spiritual gift and all others, “distributing to each one individually as He wills” (1 Cor. 12:11). Throughout time, spiritually gifted spokespersons have delivered divine messages to restore and renew our relationship with God, just as God did in Eden after the Fall.

We will explore how God calls prophets and how we can test their authenticity. We will see the similarities and differences between Old and New Testament prophets as well as gain a working understanding of how revelation and inspiration functioned in their lives. Some prophets spoke, others wrote, and some did both. We will examine these and other modes of divine revelation, their implications, and the blessings of obeying the messages these prophets delivered.

God uses the gift of prophecy for five crucial purposes: 1. To reveal Himself, His truth, and His will for fallen humanity; 2. To give insight and counsel regarding important events and happenings; 3. To equip the body of Christ—the church—for mission; 4. To provide spiritual encouragement to His followers; and 5. To awaken sinners and call them to repentance. In addition, “the spirit of prophecy” is one of two distinguishing marks of God’s end-time remnant church (Rev. 12:17; 19:10).

God is so invested in guiding the lost safely home that He provides the gift of prophecy for the last days as well (Joel 2:28–31). Seventh-day Adventists believe that Ellen G. White, cofounder of the church, exercised a modern manifestation of the prophetic gift. This belief is why we will dedicate one section of the lesson each week to explore the dynamics of Ellen White’s call to prophetic ministry. We as a church have been given this wonderful gift. How do we best use it?

From infancy onward, we have been wired to be spoken to. How important, then, that we listen to what God’s prophets tell us!
