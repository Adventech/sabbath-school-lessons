### Jesus as Prophet

Moses predicted that “the Lord your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from your midst, from your brethren. Him you shall hear” (Deut. 18:15). Who was that prophet? Scripture is clear; this prophecy pointed to Jesus Himself. Peter attested to this while preaching in Jerusalem: “For Moses truly said to the fathers, ‘The Lord your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from your brethren. Him you shall hear in all things, whatever He says to you’” (Acts 3:22). Jesus is the ultimate Prophet, bringing a message directly from heaven.

During Jesus’ ministry, whether He was the promised Prophet was widely discussed (Mark 6:14, 15; John 6:14; 7:40). Yes, Jesus was a prophet—and much more than a prophet. When Jesus questioned the disciples to test how well they understood His identity, they answered correctly that He was the Christ (Mark 8:27–29). Jesus, as the Christ, the Messiah, is the One to whom all the Old Testament prophets pointed. The New Testament recognizes Jesus as the fulfillment of the Messianic prophecies—and Jesus Himself affirmed it (Luke 24:13–35).

In John 1:1–5, the description of the Word does not fit any mere human being. The Word is identified as the Creator, for “all things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made” (John 1:3). In other words, He created everything that was created—the entire cosmos, now estimated to be at least 93 billion light-years wide. But the Word who created us and created the universe is the same Word that became flesh when Jesus came to this world (John 1:14). In every “and God said” of Genesis 1, we hear Jesus speaking. He who said, “Let there be light” (Gen. 1:3) is Himself the “light of men” (John 1:4). Through the plan of salvation (John 1:4, 5), He dispelled earth’s darkness.

God’s prophetic words are certain and confirmed. When heeded, they are “a light that shines in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts” (2 Pet. 1:19). Prophecy will continue to bring light to our sinful world until Jesus, “the Morning Star” and Source of that prophetic light, ushers in His eternal kingdom. Until then, through His life, death, and resurrection, Christ offers each of us the gift of eternal life. The only question is whether we accept it.
