As we journey through this study, it’s important to engage deeply with the material and reflect on its implications for our faith and daily lives. In your Sabbath School class (or Bible study group), use the outline of questions below to lead a thoughtful discussion based on this week’s main passage and supporting verses.

**Apostles and prophets:**

1. What was the significance of the apostles and the prophets in the early church? Eph. 2:19–22
2. How did Paul learn the mysteries of the gospel? Who revealed these special messages to the apostles and prophets? Eph. 3:3–5
3. What are the differences and similarities between apostles and prophets? See this week’s inTro section.

**Profiles of prophets and their assistants:**

1. Who were some of the female prophets in the New Testament? Luke 2:36–38; Acts 21:9
2. Were all the prophets apostles, and did all prophets write books of the Bible? Acts 15:32
3. Who were some of Paul’s assistants that he mentored? 1 Tim. 1:18; 4:12; Titus 1:4

`Personal reflection: How can you help your friends discover the gifts and talents they have that God can use in His work?`

**The true versus the false:**

1. Who was the ultimate Prophet (though He was more than a prophet)? Deut. 18:15; Acts 3:20–22
2. During His ministry, how was Jesus acknowledged as the promised Prophet? John 6:14; 7:40
3. What kind of false prophets did Jesus warn about? Why should we take this warning so seriously? Matt. 24:11, 23–25
4. What are some ways we can know the difference between true messengers of God and false messengers? Matt. 7:15–20; 1 John 4:1–3

`Personal reflection: What can you do to better guard yourself against all the deceptions sweeping our world?`

**Key takeaways to remember:**

- Guided by the Holy Spirit, both prophets and apostles received special revelations from heaven.
- Prophets are not limited to one demographic. God has used men and women, old and young alike.
- Jesus is the supreme prophet. And He warned of the grave danger of false prophets.
