### Female Prophets and Young Pastoral Associates

In Old Testament times, God called both men and women to be His messengers. There’s clear evidence of female prophets in New Testament times as well. Anna is the first one identified as a “prophetess” in the New Testament. In her full-time ministry, she “served God with fastings and prayers night and day. And . . . she gave thanks to the Lord, and spoke of Him” (Luke 2:37, 38). At age eighty-four, God spoke through her in the temple in Jerusalem when she acknowledged Baby Jesus as the Promised One.

Acts 21:9 says Philip had “four virgin daughters who prophesied.” New Testament historians such as Eusebius have found later texts that mention where they possibly lived and were buried. These were women—most likely young women—who clearly had the prophetic gift. We don’t know exactly what they prophesied or the extent of their ministry, but we do know that none of their prophetic words made it into the canon, the Bible. Like many others, they were noncanonical prophets—true prophets whose words or writings were not preserved as biblical books.

In addition to female prophets playing a role in the early church, young people also made significant contributions. Apostles often mentored young leaders for ministry. Paul, for instance, addressed Timothy as “a true son in the faith” (1 Tim. 1:2; see also 1 Tim. 4:12). Paul taught Timothy the basics of pastoral ministry. Regarding Timothy’s calling, he said, “This charge I commit to you, son Timothy, according to the prophecies previously made concerning you, that by them you may wage the good warfare” (1 Tim. 1:18). Paul entrusted young Timothy with great responsibility.

Another example comes in Titus 1:4, where Paul introduced Titus as “a true son in our common faith.” Paul gave Titus the task of appointing leaders for the believers in Crete, a challenging congregation. He offered young Titus spiritual counsel on how to approach this important responsibility, then concluded, “Speak these things, exhort, and rebuke with all authority. Let no one despise you” (Titus 2:15). With these words, Paul encouraged Titus not to be intimidated or discouraged but to lead with discernment and strength.

God used many different kinds of people in many different amazing ways in the New Testament church. Women prophesied, as did men. The more experienced and aged apostles mentored young leaders who were entrusted with important responsibilities in caring for new church plants. People from different generations, cultures, and backgrounds worked together to help God’s work move forward successfully.
