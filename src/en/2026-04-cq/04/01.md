**Read This Lesson’s Passage:** Ephesians 2:19—3:7

### The Same Engine

As a father and son headed home, the son scanned the cars they were passing, looking for those that were the same as his dad’s. The father suddenly pointed to a car and exclaimed, “That was the same as my first car!” When they got home, the father pulled out the owner’s manual of his first car along with the manual of the current car. He carefully showed his son the features of each vehicle, highlighting things that were alike and what had been changed. When they compared the two engines, everything was identical. Much had changed, but not the engine.

God spoke through prophets in both the Old and New Testaments. Continuity and harmony between both testaments exist because the Holy Spirit—the Source, or “Engine,” of divine inspiration—has remained the same. The Holy Spirit always empowers true prophets through the gift of prophecy. He also places Christ at the center of the prophets’ messages, as Hebrews makes clear (Heb. 1:1–3).

One important issue that arises when reading the New Testament is understanding the differences and similarities between prophets and apostles. The prophets in the New Testament continued many of the same functions as Old Testament prophets, which we’re quite familiar with. The word “apostle,” however, is a New Testament term first introduced in the Gospels. So, then, what made someone an apostle?

The apostles were “messengers,” or “ones sent forth” from God, as defined by the Greek word apostolos. Throughout the four Gospels, the word “apostle” refers to the twelve disciples. The rest of the New Testament supports this idea and expands it, including other church leaders who were not among Christ’s original twelve.

Paul called himself an apostle twelve times in his epistles. In Romans 1:1, 2, he connected his apostolic calling and the content of his preaching with the message of the Old Testament prophets. He presented himself as an apostle to the Gentiles (Rom. 11:13).

Paul was appointed “through the will of God” (1 Cor. 1:1). Unlike the priesthood, which followed a familial line from fathers to sons, God appointed prophets and apostles according to His will. When Paul’s apostleship was in dispute, he defended his calling (1 Cor. 9:1, 2). Paul saw his apostleship as building on the work of Christ, the Foundation of the church (Eph. 2:20). As recipients of God’s revelation, the apostles possessed the same level of inspiration as the prophets (Eph. 3:1–5).

In 2 Peter 3:1, 2, Peter spoke of prophets as those who came before and whose words are important, but then presented the apostles as carrying the same authority. In these verses, both Old and New Testament times are referenced. While prophets may seem to be God’s messengers of the Old Testament era and apostles God’s messengers of the New Testament era, there were some people who prophesied or exercised the prophetic gift but were not apostles. To put it another way, the gift of prophecy was not limited to the apostles in New Testament times, and not all prophets were apostles (Acts 15:32).

Without the gift of prophecy being given to the apostles and other prophets, there could be no story of an early church taking the gospel to the world. Only through the power and guidance of the Holy Spirit could the church accomplish its mission. Through dreams and revelations, God told His messengers where to go (Acts 10:1–23; 16:6–10). Inspired by the Holy Spirit, the prophets remembered Jesus’ words and wrote down the story of the Gospels for all the world to read (John 14:26). The Holy Spirit taught them important truths and revealed the future to them (John 16:13; Rev. 1:1–3).

In reading the New Testament, we note that Jesus was the center of almost everything they wrote. In one way or another, His life, death, resurrection, high-priestly ministry, and Second Coming were their central focus.

This week, we will examine the gift of prophecy in the New Testament, looking at what prophets did, who functioned as prophets, how prophets compared to apostles, and the danger of false prophets.
