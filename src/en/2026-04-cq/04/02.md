### Functions of New Testament Prophets

Much of the ministry of the New Testament prophets reflected that of the Old Testament prophets. Just as God instructed and guided His people via prophets in the Old Testament, so He used prophets in the New Testament to guide the establishment and growth of His church. The gift of prophecy given to the apostles and prophets was essential for the formation and development of the early church (Eph. 2:20). Paul was one such apostle who received divine revelations (Eph. 3:3). The Holy Spirit revealed to him and other prophets the gospel of Jesus Christ to share with all the world (Eph. 3:5). These divinely inspired messages were shared in person with audiences at that time and written down and preserved in the books of the New Testament. Thus, the messages the Holy Spirit revealed to the prophets became foundational to the future of the church.

Ephesians 4:11–13 provides a list of spiritual gifts that are important for the church’s success. Prophets are mentioned among apostles, evangelists, pastors, and teachers (Eph. 4:11). These persons exist for “the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ, till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ” (Eph. 4:12, 13). As seen here and elsewhere, Jesus is the central focus of the prophetic messages.

New Testament prophets also provided the church with encouragement, which is mentioned in the context of prophesying (1 Cor. 14:31). The book of Acts talked about two prophets, Judas and Silas (neither of whom wrote a book in the Bible), who “exhorted and strengthened the brethren with many words” (Acts 15:32). Though prophets sometimes have to give stern warnings, they also offer words of encouragement, especially in trying times. These encouragements were no doubt greatly appreciated by those who needed to hear those words and the hope and promise they conveyed. In times of tribulation and persecution in particular, the gift of prophecy has been a source of tremendous encouragement and hope.

In Revelation 22:6, Jesus is presented as “Lord God of the holy prophets,” whose “words are faithful and true.” Of course, anything that comes from God’s spokespersons is also going to be “faithful and true.” In a world in which we can trust so little, we can indeed trust what the prophets of the Lord say, especially in His Word, our final and ultimate authority.
