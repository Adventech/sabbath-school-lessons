### Understanding the Prophets

In New Testament times, many foreigners traveled long distances to visit Jerusalem and seek spiritual renewal. Acts 8 tells of one such visitor from Ethiopia who “had come to Jerusalem to worship” (Acts 8:27). This man was familiar with the Jewish religion and with at least some of its ceremonies. He even owned a copy of the writings of Isaiah the prophet, which he was avidly reading during his trip home. Yet he was perplexed by the prophecy of the sin-bearing Servant found in Isaiah 53. This prophecy remained a mystery to him. He longed for someone to explain the meaning of Isaiah’s message and guide his understanding.

In answer to this man’s prayer, an angel sent Philip to help this traveler. Hearing him read from the book of Isaiah, Philip asked if he understood what he was reading. The man acknowledged his helplessness and his desire to have someone explain it. Philip explained to his new friend how Jesus was rejected, scorned, unjustly tried, condemned, and executed as a criminal in fulfillment of Isaiah’s prophecy. With great delight, he showed how Jesus died for our sins according to this prophecy and other scriptures.

This experience teaches us a few important lessons. First, the simple reading of a passage of Scripture does not always lead to a deeper understanding of that passage. We need a proper understanding of the background and sufficient knowledge to discern the meaning of a passage. Second, in the study of Scripture, we can and should learn from each other. The Bible is not intended to be studied only in isolation. Third, we have a personal obligation to share sound Bible messages with others. God could use angels, but He sends people to help other people understand His Word.

Philip’s exposition of the book of Isaiah and other Old Testament passages convicted the man and led him to desire baptism (Acts 8:35–39; compare with Ps. 51:12). Our expositions of Scripture should have the same purpose (see John 17:17).

This story provides an example of what our Bible study should be. It should be focused on two main purposes: personal spiritual growth and leading others into a saving relationship with Jesus. Christ defined the true Christian as one who is guided by the Holy Spirit “into all truth” (John 16:13), who lives “by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God” (Matt. 4:4), and who teaches others “all things” He has commanded us (Matt. 28:20).

**Questions to explore:**

`Even though we sometimes need help to understand the Bible, why must we always be convinced in our own minds of what we believe?`

`Think of a time you didn’t understand something in the Bible, and it greatly bothered you until someone explained it to you. How can that experience help you trust God with the things in His Word that, for now, you still don’t understand?`
