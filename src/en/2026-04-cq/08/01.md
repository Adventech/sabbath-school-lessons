**Read This Week’s Passage:** Acts 8:26–40

### The Meaning of the Text

Prophetic writings never come with a guarantee that everyone will properly interpret the message. Hearing various people interpret the same message in very different ways can be confusing. How a person interprets a prophetic message is highly consequential. Correctly interpreting prophetic writings points people in the right direction, but a false understanding of prophecy can lead to spiritual disaster.

In the aftermath of His crucifixion and resurrection, Christ appeared to two downcast disciples, both of whom confessed, “We were hoping that it was He who was going to redeem Israel” (Luke 24:21). The disciples expected a Messiah who would deliver the Jews from the Roman occupation. This false expectation and consequent disappointment were derived from a biased, one-sided interpretation of the Messianic prophecies of the Old Testament.

Christ resolved these two disciples’ misunderstanding by explaining the Scriptures to them from a broader perspective (Luke 24:25–27). Christ’s interpretation of Scripture opened their eyes and made their hearts burn within them (Luke 24:31, 32). Later, Jesus appeared to a larger group of disciples in Jerusalem, and “He opened their understanding, that they might comprehend the Scriptures” (Luke 24:45; see also Luke 24:33, 36, 44–49).

These experiences highlight the importance of correctly understanding prophetic writings. The events surrounding Christ’s death confused and deeply troubled the disciples. They were familiar with the scriptures foretelling the Messiah’s suffering and death, yet they missed the implications until Christ showed them.

Like the disciples, we too must seek a true understanding of Scripture. All Christians should understand the Bible for themselves. The Bible is clear enough for all people to understand the way of salvation, yet complex enough to consistently challenge the brightest minds. Scripture is an endless treasure of saving knowledge that can never be fully exhausted.

Some people claim the Bible does not need to be interpreted. If this is the case, why then did Jesus ask the lawyer, “What’s written in God’s Law? How do you interpret it” (Luke 10:26, MSG)? Jesus answered people’s questions by directing them to relevant teachings of Scripture and probing their interpretation.

How we interpret the meaning of a text determines the conclusions we come to—whether right or wrong. To grasp a text’s meaning, we should first understand the words used, then how they’re used within the literary context and the overall biblical context, and also the historical and cultural setting. Finally, our conclusions should be in full harmony with the wider teachings of Scripture. Comparing parallel scriptures can enrich our understanding.

A popular preacher’s anecdote tells of a man who was facing serious problems and wanted to know God’s will for him. He opened his Bible and pointed his finger randomly to Matthew 27:5, which reads, “And [Judas] went and hanged himself.” Taken aback by the verse, he repeated the same procedure, attempting to find a more uplifting message, only to have his finger rest on Luke 10:37: “Then Jesus said to him, ‘Go and do likewise.’” A final attempt led him to John 13:27: “Then Jesus said to him, ‘What you do, do quickly.’” All these are true verses of Scripture, but they must be understood in their contexts.

Indeed, many distortions of Bible messages are derived from the common practice of isolating passages from their literary context. This approach opens the Scriptures to all kinds of false interpretations. Some people distort the Bible’s message simply because of their own lack of knowledge. However, others do so intentionally, as Satan did in both the Garden of Eden and while tempting Jesus in the wilderness. Regardless of the motivation, the result is always detrimental.

Among the most important characteristics of a faithful interpreter of Scripture is a combination of thoroughness and humility. Remember, none of us owns truth; we all “see through a glass darkly” (1 Cor 13:12, KJV).

We should always strive for sound doctrine (2 Tim. 4:1–5). A crucial principle to follow is to focus on what we do understand, on what’s clear, and to build from that, which often, over time, will help us understand things that now might be confusing. Short of eternity, there will always be unanswered questions.
