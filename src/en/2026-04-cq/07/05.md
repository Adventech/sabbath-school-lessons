### For Our Admonition

The accounts in the Old Testament, however sordid and sad they may be, were given for a good reason: so we can learn lessons about what happens when we rebel against God. According to the apostle Paul, God preserved these important stories for our instruction, to save us from repeating the same mistakes: “Now these things became our examples, to the intent that we should not lust after evil things as they [our forebears] also lusted” (1 Cor. 10:6). In other words, the past sets the context for what happens now and in the future, and unless we learn from it, we’re bound to repeat it.

In reference to the Israelites’ experiences and failures during their years of wilderness wandering, Paul concluded, “Now all these things happened to them as examples, and they were written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the ages have come” (1 Cor. 10:11). The past record of humanity’s experiences with God—both good and bad—is not to be overlooked or discounted. He draws a straight line connecting the past and the present, linking ancient Israel and the present church of which we who have accepted Jesus as Lord and Savior are now a part (Gal. 6:14–16).

In effect, God is saying we are not doomed to repeat the mistakes of past generations so long as we heed the admonitions of Scripture and the messages He sends us through His prophets. The stories and writings of the prophets should move us to be humble and rely on God. As Paul said, “Therefore let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall” (1 Cor. 10:12). God provides the strength for us to learn from the past and experience victory today (1 Cor. 10:13). If we pay attention, learn, and obey, our lives will be established and we will prosper (2 Chron. 20:20). The spiritual prosperity God wants to give us is not defined by having nice homes, cars, clothing, and other material possessions. While God promises to take care of our needs, He especially wants our souls to prosper, even as we prosper in other ways (3 John 2). However, true, lasting spiritual fullness in the Christian life requires obedience to God’s messages.

God does not waste words. When He speaks, His words are eternal. Their power and efficacy never end (Isa. 40:8). They are given to admonish us, “upon whom the ends of the ages have come.” If that was true back then, how much more do these words apply to us today?
