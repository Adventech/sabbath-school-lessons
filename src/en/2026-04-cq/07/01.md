**Read This Week’s Passage:** Jeremiah 36:20–32

### Anger at God’s Message

Some people today are encouraged to “speak their truth”—their experiences, their ideas, their needs, their boundaries, their convictions—whatever these may be. As per the thinking, every “truth” is valid and should not be silenced. What happens, however, when some of these deeply held “truths” conflict with the Truth, Jesus Christ, and His Written Word?

When faced with this contradiction, some people accept biblical truths and turn away from their errors, but others cling to their lies. Some change their behavior in response to truth; others rebel against it. Such is the history of humanity.

Biblical prophets were often called to deliver messages that people did not want to hear. As a result, many were criticized, ostracized, even threatened with death or indeed killed (Heb. 11:32–38). Despite all this, God’s prophets faithfully proclaimed their messages because divine prophetic guidance was not a calling they chose. It was lifesaving instruction for the people of God. The messages had to be delivered, no matter the personal cost to the prophet.

The very founding of Israel as an independent nation began with prophets challenging the popular sentiments of the people. Moses was a leader and a prophet who learned very intimately the joys and pains of guiding a mixed multitude as he led the Israelites through the wilderness in search of the Promised Land.

When the Israelite spies brought back reports of terrifying giants and huge walled cities in Canaan, the people panicked (Num 13:31—14:1). Turning against Moses and Aaron, the multitude demanded new leadership who would take them back to Egypt (Num. 14:2–4). Moses and Aaron, the human representatives of God’s ultimate Lordship, were easy targets for the nation’s anger. The need for physical and mental safety is a core motivation for all human beings. When either is threatened, or we perceive something as a threat, we can become irate or even irrational.

The Israelites harbored legitimate fears about the challenges that lay ahead in possessing the Promised Land, “but when men yield their hearts to unbelief they place themselves under the control of Satan, and none can tell to what lengths he will lead them.”<sup>1</sup> Moses faced a nearly insurmountable leadership challenge in that moment. The people’s fear soon became anger against Aaron and Moses and open rebellion against God.

Despite the skepticism and rebellion pervading the multitude, there were faithful people like Joshua and Caleb who trusted God implicitly. When the people sought new leaders to replace those chosen by God, Joshua and Caleb tore their clothes and pleaded with the people to trust in God despite their fears, saying, “If the Lord delights in us, then He will bring us into this land and give it to us, ‘a land which flows with milk and honey.’ Only do not rebel against the Lord, nor fear the people of the land” (Num. 14:8, 9). These faithful voices infuriated the people, which would’ve resulted in Joshua’s and Caleb’s deaths if not for God’s intervention (Num. 14:10).

The messages delivered by God’s prophets consistently challenge people to reconsider their opinions and trust God’s guidance. The conflict between Moses and the multitude is a tension felt in many stories of the Old Testament. This struggle is on full display in the prophetic ministry of Jeremiah.

When God called Jeremiah, He called him to a difficult ministry that very few people would agree to. God told him, “Therefore prepare yourself and arise, And speak to them all that I command you. Do not be dismayed before their faces, Lest I dismay you before them” (Jer. 1:17). God told Jeremiah that to be a prophet, he must be as strong as an iron pillar—dependable and unmovable (Jer. 1:18). Indeed, Jeremiah faced harsh opposition during his prophetic ministry, but God upheld him and gave him the strength needed for his calling.
