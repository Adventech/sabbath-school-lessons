### Destroying the Message

Jeremiah was God’s prophetic voice to the nation of Judah during a time of impending doom. For many years, God had been sending messages through Jeremiah to warn Judah—to tell them that He was displeased with them and that Babylon would conquer them and take them captive into exile (Jer. 6:8–19, 26). The Lord was seeking to save them, but first they had to obey.

Judah’s sins were many, open, and unchanging. Idolatry, child sacrifice, adultery, and tyranny were common and widespread during Jeremiah’s time, yet the prophet’s warnings and appeals were not heeded. As Jeremiah’s prophetic ministry neared its end, God’s appeals to His wayward children grew more intense, yet His efforts always remained redemptive. In Jeremiah 32, for instance, God tells Jeremiah to buy a piece of land and hold on to the deed, “For thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel: ‘Houses and fields and vineyards shall be possessed again in this land’” (Jer. 32:15). Buying this property demonstrated the prophet’s faith that God’s people would return to their land after the Babylonian exile.

It was love that moved God to have Jeremiah write a kind of final appeal for repentance and reformation. Baruch wrote out his message and read it aloud in Jerusalem. The message startled those who were listening. When the princes in the royal court heard the Jeremiah’s message, they summoned Baruch to come and read the scroll to them. The princes immediately recognized that Jeremiah would be in danger when they shared this scroll with the king. Concerned for his safety, they warned Baruch to take Jeremiah and go into hiding (Jer. 36:19).

The princes brought their report to King Jehoiakim, who demanded that the scroll be brought and read to him. The king heard only the beginning of Jeremiah’s message before he flew into a rage, grabbed the manuscript, cut it up, and threw it into the fire (Jer. 36:20–23). Jehoiakim’s response was one of disdain for God, His message, and His messenger.

Sometimes there’s a tendency to believe that if we dismiss or destroy God’s messages or His messengers, the message itself will vanish. Our unwillingness to heed God’s prophetic Word does not erase the message He has for us. Our disdain only intensifies the harm that will befall us and our families because of our willful ignorance or rebellion. Jeremiah’s prophecy about King Jehoiakim’s end holds a grave warning for every human being (Jer. 36:30, 31): we ignore God’s warnings to us at our own peril, for we will reap the consequences of our sin and evil.

**Questions to explore:**

`What does God’s persistence in trying to reach His people despite their defiance tell us about His loving character?`

`Imagine if King Jehoiakim had accepted Jeremiah’s message. How might that have changed Jerusalem’s story?`
