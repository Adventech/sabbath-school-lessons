### A History of Resistance

Jeremiah’s prophetic ministry spanned the reigns of Josiah, Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, and Zedekiah. The kingdom of Babylon, led by Nebuchadnezzar, had already robbed Jerusalem and taken some captives back to Babylon when a twentyone- year-old named Zedekiah came to the throne of Judah (2 Chron. 36:11). By this time, Jeremiah’s prophecies were nearly fulfilled. Of this new king, the Bible says, “He did evil in the sight of the Lord his God, and did not humble himself before Jeremiah the prophet, who spoke from the mouth of the Lord” (2 Chron. 36:12). Notice how Jeremiah’s prophetic gift is described: he’s portrayed as speaking out of the very mouth of God. Nevertheless, Zedekiah did not humble himself before the prophet.

Jeremiah, who was only a man, a sinner like us, was a representative of the Lord and spoke on His behalf. Jeremiah was one among many in that role. For centuries, God sent prophets to His people (Exod. 3:15; Ps. 105:26; Mal. 3:1; Isa. 6:8; Jer. 25:4; Matt. 11:13; Luke 11:49; etc.), “not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance” (2 Pet. 3:9). The history of ancient Israel and Judah is the story of God’s ceaseless attempts to reach His backsliding people with His promises of salvation, if only they would repent.

At this late hour in Judah’s history, the grace God extended to Judah through the guidance and comfort of Jeremiah’s prophetic voice was met not with humility, faith, and repentance but with laughter, mockery, and derision.

The human response to the gift of prophecy can take many forms. With rare exceptions, the history of ancient Israel and Judah was one of rejecting the prophets. In Moses’ time, the children of Israel fell into open rebellion against him and Aaron, driven by fear of the foes they would encounter when trying to enter the Promised Land. King Jehoiakim followed the pattern of his fathers when he burned God’s message in hopes of avoiding God’s prophetic word. Prophets before and after Jeremiah met similar types of resistance.

This long history of God’s people rejecting the prophets led them to persecute Jeremiah and finally resulted in Judah’s captivity to Babylon (2 Chron. 36:15–21). This is the tragic storyline that grieved Jesus so much when He cried out, “O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the one who kills the prophets and stones those who are sent to her!” (Matt. 23:37). This sad history weighed on the heart of Stephen when he asked the Jewish council at Jerusalem, “Which of the prophets did your fathers not persecute?” (Acts 7:52). Even mentioning this history was intolerable to the national leaders, who interrupted Stephen’s speech and stoned him to death (Acts 7:57, 58). The history of God’s people in both the Old and New Testament show that people often struggle to accept the messages and warnings God sends through the prophets.
