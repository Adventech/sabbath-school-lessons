### Divine Messages and Human Imperfections

Prophetic messages come to us through a combination of divine and human influences. God speaks through prophets as they employ imperfect human language to communicate God’s perfect thoughts. Paul described this coming together of the earthly and the heavenly when he wrote, “We have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellence of the power may be of God and not of us” (2 Cor. 4:7). God could have delivered His treasured truths directly through angels (heavenly vessels), but instead entrusted them to “earthen vessels” (human beings), so that His power might be shown in contrast to our own weaknesses. Scripture reveals that just as God’s messengers were not supernaturally preserved from failings in their personal lives (e.g., Moses, Elijah, David, Jonah, Peter), so their writings were not exempt from the imperfections of human understanding and communication.

Just as the incarnate Jesus had both a divine nature and a human nature, so does the Bible. “The union of the divine and the human, manifest in Christ, exists also in the Bible. The truths revealed are all ‘given by inspiration of God;’ yet they are expressed in the words of men and are adapted to human needs.”<sup>1</sup>

The humanity of God’s messengers is not overridden by their divine calling to communicate the treasures of truth, as Paul wrote in 2 Corinthians 4:7. Additionally, the Holy Spirit did not prevent His messengers from making statements and writing words that today we find hard to harmonize or explain. However few these verses are, it takes a very narrow mind to stumble over them. As Bible-believing Christians, we are to submit to what we do understand of God’s Word, and to trust the Lord despite what we don’t understand.

Some discrepancies in the Bible can be attributed to inconsequential mistakes by later copyists or to conflicting sources that the inspired authors relied on. When considering problematic passages, we need to ask ourselves if we fully understand the meaning of the writer’s words and whether further study might help us understand what we’re missing.

Even if we’re unable to resolve every seeming mistake or discrepancy, they’re inconsequential to the essence of the message being presented. We can have confidence that the divine truths of Scripture are faithfully revealed despite the imperfections of God’s messengers.

**Questions to consider:**

`Why do you think God chose to use fallible human beings rather than sinless angels as His primary messengers?`

`Journal a personal prayer to God.`
