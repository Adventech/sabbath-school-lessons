**Read This Week’s Passage:** Jeremiah 36:1–19

### Literary Genres and Styles

The Bible describes three major ways prophets communicated God’s messages: orally, through writing, and through symbolic actions or dramatizations. This week we will focus on the prophetic writings of Scripture. The Bible records the Word of God as expressed through the words of His messengers, including sermons and speeches of the prophets. The earliest known method God used to convey messages to humanity was through direct oral communication, such as audible voices, dreams, or angels. It was not until the time of Moses that Scripture records the first divine instruction to write down what God had spoken (Exod. 17:14).

In the Old Testament, we find multiple examples of God giving directions to His messengers to preserve His words for His people. Like Moses, Jeremiah was told, “Take a scroll of a book and write on it all the words that I have spoken to you against Israel, against Judah, and against all the nations, from the day I spoke to you, from the days of Josiah even to this day” (Jer. 36:2). Jeremiah was also told why he was to write out the prophecies: “It may be that the house of Judah will hear all the adversities which I purpose to bring upon them, that everyone may turn from his evil way, that I may forgive their iniquity and their sin” (Jer. 36:3). The prophetic writings were given to lead the people to salvation, not simply for the sake of curiosity about the future.

God’s messengers wrote under a great variety of circumstances. Moses wrote the first five books of the Bible while in the desert. David composed several of his psalms while being hunted by Saul (see Psalms 52, 54, 57, 59). Daniel wrote his visions while serving in the courts of Babylon and Medo-Persia. Paul wrote his letters to Philemon and the Ephesians, Philippians, and Colossians while he was in prison, and John wrote Revelation while exiled on Patmos. It’s evident that when God’s messengers were impressed to write, they were empowered by the Holy Spirit regardless of their circumstances.

Interestingly, all of Jesus’ preserved teachings come to us in written form through people who God chose. This shows the confidence God had that divine messages would be faithfully recorded and preserved by His messengers.

The Bible contains a variety of writing styles. Some passages resemble the writing of a diary (e.g., Num. 33:1–49), and many feature common literary devices such as metaphors (symbols) and hyperbole (exaggeration). Some, like the books of Daniel, Zechariah, and John, contain examples of apocalyptic writing, which is marked by characteristics such as grand symbolism, cosmic themes, and descriptions of heavenly beings.

Many portions of the Bible are written in poetic form, often using parallelism, which is when subsequent lines repeat or sometimes contrast with earlier lines. For example: “In that day a man will look to his Maker, and his eyes will have respect for the Holy One of Israel” (Isa. 17:7). “Maker” and “Holy One” parallel each other, each image giving us more information about the other.

Another literary style frequently found in Scripture is the “covenant lawsuit.” For example, in Jeremiah 2:4–9, Jeremiah presented God’s legal complaint against His people for violating their covenantal obligations. Using courtroom imagery, the author portrayed God as the prosecutor laying out the reasons for His judgment.

Scripture also contains prophetic writing, history, songs, and parables. Jeremiah was told to compose a book of all his messages (Jer. 36:2), while much of the New Testament writings consist of letters addressed to individuals or communities. Despite the vast differences between these literary genres and styles, there is no distinction in the authority of one style of communication over another. Paul’s letters were regarded as carrying the same weight as the prophets of old because Paul gave clear evidence in his letters that he was inspired by the Holy Spirit (see 1 Thess. 2:13). Though Scripture includes many different genres and styles of literature, it is all inspired by the same Spirit and speaks with one voice.
