As we journey through this study, it’s important to engage deeply with the material and reflect on its implications for our faith and daily lives. In your Sabbath School class (or Bible study group), use the outline of questions below to lead a thoughtful discussion based on this week’s main passage and supporting verses.

**Using assistants:**

1. How did Baruch assist Jeremiah in writing his book and sharing it with his audience? Jer. 36:1–6, 14–17
2. Who helped Paul write Romans? Rom. 16:22

`Personal reflection: How far would the prophets’ messages have gone without the aid of literary assistants and the later work of copyists, translators, and publishers? What role can we play today to help their messages reach more people?`

**Quoting from other sources:**

1. Did Solomon compose all his own proverbs, or did he use others’ materials as well? Eccles. 12:9, 10
2. How did prophetic writings reference other historical sources? 1 Kings 11:41; Ezra 4–7
3. What quotes did Paul use from Greek philosophers? Acts 17:28; Titus 1:12

`Personal reflection: How do you respond when you encounter something in the Bible that appears to be a contradiction or mistake of some kind?`

**The treasure of truth:**

1. Rather than revealing treasured truth through heavenly vessels (angels), who does God use? 2 Cor. 4:7
2. In what way does “the Word” unite divinity with humanity? John 1:14
3. How did Jesus often encourage people to read the prophets to find authoritative answers to their questions? See the list of verses in this week’s inSpect.

`Personal reflection: Look at your Bible. How might it be different if angels, instead of humans, had written and delivered it to you?`

**Key takeaways to remember:**

- Some Bible prophets partnered with literary assistants who helped them write their books and deliver their messages.
- The Bible sometimes quotes from extrabiblical documents and literature.
- God could’ve used angels to write and share His messages, but He chose imperfect people instead.
