Arthur White was Ellen G. White’s grandson. In the past, Arthur served as the manager for his grandmother’s writings. Arthur believed that God gave his grandmother hundreds of dreams during her lifetime. Some dreams continued for a few minutes. Other dreams continued for many hours. At times, many people saw what happened to Ellen White when God gave her a dream. Some people were there who didn’t believe Ellen White was God’s messenger. God wanted them to watch what happened to her when He gave her a dream.

In 1868, James White wrote about what happened to his wife when God gave her a special message: “She didn’t know what was happening around her.” She didn’t breathe. Her muscles became stiff. But when she moved a part of her body, she moved it easily. Her eyes were wide open. When the dream ended, she was blind for a little while. But nothing was wrong with her eyes. No one controlled when her dreams happened or how long they continued. Even Ellen White herself didn’t know how long the dream would be. (Read James White, Life Incidents, pages 271–274.)

As in Bible times, some people accepted the messages. Other people rejected them. Ellen White said, “God chose me to warn His people. . . . If they don’t accept His advice, He will hold them responsible. . . . In the name and strength of my Savior, I will warn people. I shall give them His message and try to encourage them. I shall do whatever God’s Spirit leads me to do. Maybe people will hear and obey. Or maybe they will not. My job is not to make myself happy. My job is to do what My Father in heaven wants me to do. He gave me my work.”—Testimonies for the Church, volume 4, pages 231, 232, adapted.

Ellen White was very kind to people who didn’t believe in her gift. She said that these people must be shown “love. We must be patient with them while they decide what to believe.”—Testimonies for the Church, volume 1, page 328, adapted. Ellen White believed that her messages were proof that her gift was from God. If people loved Bible truth, they would accept her gift. Ellen White trusted God to give these people understanding. She also wrote that God “gives us enough proof for our faith.”—Testimonies for the Church, volume 4, page 231, adapted.
