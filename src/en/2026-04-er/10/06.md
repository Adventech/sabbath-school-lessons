date:   03/12/2026
---

`Read 1 Chronicles 29:29 and 2 Chronicles 9:29. What do you learn about some of the messengers in these verses?`

There were many Bible messengers whose books didn’t become part of the Bible. These messengers included Nathan, Gad, Ahijah, and Iddo.

`Read 1 Kings 11:29–39 and Luke 7:28. What do these verses teach us about the power of messengers whose writings are not included in the Bible?`

Why did God allow the writings of some messengers to be included in the Bible and not the writings of others? We don’t know. What we do know is that the messengers whose writings were not included in the Bible were filled with the Holy Spirit. Their messages fully came from God. The Bible ­teaches us that the spiritual gifts will continue in the church until the end times. That includes the gift of special messages. The Bible says that there will be special messengers from God in the end times. Paul says, the Lord “gave all these gifts to help God’s people to serve him in different ways” (Ephesians 4:12, EASY). Paul adds, “This work must continue until we are all joined together in what we believe and in what we know about the Son of God” (Ephesians 4:13, ERV). For sure, the spiritual gifts will continue in the church until “the great [amazing] and terrible day of the Lord comes” (Joel 2:31, NIrV).

After David slept with Bathsheba and then killed her husband, God sent Nathan to talk to him (2 Samuel 12:7–14). Nathan didn’t write a book of the Bible. But David didn’t question Nathan’s word. Why? Because Nathan’s message came from the Holy Spirit. Nathan respected Bible truth. Modern messengers will also respect it, too. Their messages must be tested with the Bible. Modern messages don’t replace the Bible as the reason for our faith and teachings.

`Ellen G. White wrote, “The Lord wants you to study your Bibles. He didn’t give us any new light to replace His Written Word. The Bible will help people who are confused to understand His truth. The Bible gives us life, the same as food.”—Selected Messages, book 3, page 29, adapted. How well does the church follow this advice?`
