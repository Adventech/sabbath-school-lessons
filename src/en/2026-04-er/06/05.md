`“The Teacher was wise man. He also taught the people to understand things. He studied many proverbs [wise teachings] and he wrote them down carefully. The Teacher tried to find the right [best] words, so that he could show the true [correct] meaning of what he wrote” (Ecclesiastes 12:9, 10, EASY).`

Solomon wrote Ecclesiastes 12:9, 10. These verses show us that Solomon didn’t invent all the wise teachings and sayings that he wrote. The Holy Spirit showed Solomon which teachings were correct. Then God’s Spirit helped Solomon to write these ideas in the best words. Proverbs 31 is an example. This chapter includes the sayings of the mother of King Lemuel (Proverbs 31:1). Again, Solomon didn’t write these sayings. But he included them in his book. History writers of the Old Testament also used information from other writers to tell the story of God’s people.

`Read 1 Kings 11:41; Luke 1:1–4; and Jude 14, 15. What do these verses tell us about how Bible writers, at times, used information from writings, teachings, and sayings outside the Bible?`

Ezra and Nehemiah are other examples of Bible writers who used history records to help write their books. Ezra includes copies of letters and laws about Jerusalem and Persia (read Ezra 4–7). Nehemiah tells us about the records of family lines that he used in his story (read Nehemiah 7:5–72).

New Testament writers also used information from outside the Bible to write their books and letters. Luke is an example. “The Holy Spirit helped Luke write his book about Jesus. Luke studied and collected information and stories about Jesus’ life. Then Luke combined this information into a story.”—The SDA Bible Commentary, volume 5, page 669, adapted.

Many people think Jude used quotes from 1 Enoch 1:9. This book is not part of the Bible. Also, Paul used quotes from Greek thinkers (read Acts 17:28 and Titus 1:12).
