Ellen G. White’s experience and writings show us much of the same things that we studied this week about God’s messengers in Bible times. God also told Ellen White to write down what He showed her.

In 1909, Ellen White thought back over her life. She wrote: “When I was a girl, I was hurt in an accident. This accident almost killed me. I was weak. I couldn’t write. . . . But one night the Lord’s angel came to me and stood next to my bed. He said to me: ‘You must write the things I give you.’ I said, ‘I can’t.’ Again, the angel commanded me, ‘Write the things I give you.’ I thought I would try. I took a lap desk from the table. I started to write. I could write easily.”—The General Conference Bulletin, May 31, 1909, adapted.

As we saw this week, the Bible writers used writing assistants. So did Ellen White. Her secretaries helped her get her writing ready to be printed. Sometimes these assistants helped her write down her thoughts. Or they read what she wrote in her own handwriting. They ­corrected misspelled words and helped Ellen White say her messages clearly. But these assistants didn’t change her thoughts or add their own.

Ellen White also used quotes and information from other books. She used these things to help her write the messages God gave her. Ellen White told her readers the names of some of the books she used. She didn’t try to hide from anyone that she used their work. She used their ideas because she found something in their writings that helped her communicate God’s truths.

Was she perfect? Did she make no mistakes? Of course not!

She wrote, “I have never said that I was perfect. Only God is perfect.”—Selected Messages, book 1, page 37, adapted.

Sometimes we may read things that Ellen White wrote that are more difficult to understand than other things she wrote. But that’s because she was human, just as we are. She didn’t become perfect or stop being human just because God chose her to be His messenger. Ellen White grew as a person and a writer, just as we all must grow. We see this growth and change in her writings over the years.
