title:  Writing Assistants
date:   03/11/2026

`Read Jeremiah 36:17, 18 and Romans 16:22. What do these verses tell us about the Bible writers and their use of writing assistants?`

Jeremiah chose to use Baruch, a writing assistant that he trusted. Baruch helped Jeremiah write God’s messages and share them with Israel’s leaders. Jeremiah isn’t the only example in the Bible of a messenger who used the help of a writing assistant to communicate God’s message.

In the New Testament, Tertius helped Paul write his letter to the Romans (Romans 16:22). Tertius was Paul’s secretary. Tertius wrote only what Paul wished to say.

Paul often used writing assistants. At the end of Paul’s letters to the Colossians and Thessalonians, Paul adds a greeting in his own handwriting (Colossians 4:18, 2 Thessalonians 3:17). Paul’s handwriting is the same as a stamp or a signature. It is proof that Paul wrote the letter. This information helps us to understand that someone else wrote down the letters to the Colossians and Thessalonians for Paul. Paul knew that some people would think that his letter didn’t come from him at all. So, Paul added a note in his own handwriting at the end of the letter. Then no one would have any reason to doubt the letter really came from him.

Some people wonder why the language is so different in many of Paul’s letters. These people believe the reason is because Paul used different assistants to help him write each letter. The different assistants each used a different type of writing to communicate Paul’s ideas. People also think the same thing about Peter’s two letters. “Yes, the language in 2 Peter is different from the language in 1 Peter. We can explain why the language is different. We think Peter used a writing assistant to help him write his letter in the Greek language. For sure, these Greek-speaking assistants chose words to help Paul and Peter communicate their messages. The word choices of the different assistants help explain why the language in each of their letters is often different.”—The SDA Bible Commentary, volume 5, page 186, adapted. So, the skills of the different writing assistants help explain why the letters are often different. Remember, too, that God’s Spirit sends the thoughts. His messengers choose the words.
