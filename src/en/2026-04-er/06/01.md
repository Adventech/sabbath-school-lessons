### Read for This Week’s Lesson
Exodus 17:14; Jeremiah 36:17, 18; Isaiah 13:1; 1 Kings 11:41; Ecclesiastes 12:9, 10; 2 Corinthians 4:7.

> <p>Memory Verse</p>
> “Everything that people wrote in the Bible in past times is there to teach us something. Those words help us to be patient and strong. As a result [for that reason], we can trust God to do what he has promised” (Romans 15:4, EASY).

Would you like to learn a song that God wrote? In Deuteronomy 32, Moses wrote a song that God taught him (Deuteronomy 31:19–22). We don’t often think about songs as messages from God about the future. But many songs in the book of Psalms really come from God. Of course, God didn’t tell His messengers the exact words to write. But God did give His messengers the ideas for the songs. Then they chose the words and often set them to music.

The Bible talks about three ways that God’s messengers communicated His messages: (1) they spoke God’s messages; (2) they wrote them down; and (3) they showed them with actions. This week, we will look at #2: the writings in the Bible. The Bible is the Written Word of God. God’s messengers wrote His ideas in their own words. God’s Words include the sermons and talks that His messengers gave.

This week, we will look at the writings of God’s messengers. Many of them used assistants or secretaries to help them write. We also will learn some important rules for Bible study. We will need to use these rules when we study verses that are hard to understand.
