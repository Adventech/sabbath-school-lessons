---
title:  Ellen G. White’s Thoughts About How God Communicates With His Messengers
date:   30/10/2026
---

Seventh-day Adventists believe that Ellen G. White was God’s messenger. Her writings, life, and work show us that God chose her to announce and write His messages. The Holy Spirit helped her write and talk in the same way He helped the Bible messengers. But Ellen White didn’t believe her writings were the same as the Bible. So, what did she think about her work as God’s messenger? What did she believe about how God communicated with His messengers?

First, Ellen White always believed the Bible was God’s Word. The Bible shows God’s truth and His plan to save sinners. God used His messengers to communicate His truth and His plan.

Second, Ellen White understood that God worked together with His messengers. God communicated His thoughts or ideas to His messengers. The messenger used his or her words to announce God’s message. As Ellen White says, “God did not choose the words for His messengers. God chose the messengers. His Spirit helped them to write God’s messages. The Spirit filled them with God’s thoughts. . . . God combines His mind with the human mind. In this way, the words of the man are the words of God.”—Ellen G. White, Selected Messages, book 1, page 21, adapted.

Ellen White also wrote, “The Bible is God-given truths written in human language. The Bible shows us how God works together with people.”—Selected Messages, book 1, page 25, adapted. God used many ways to help His messengers understand His messages. He showed them pictures in their minds. He spoke words to them and filled their minds with thoughts. Ellen White’s experience with getting messages from God was much the same as the experience of the Bible writers.

Third, Ellen White understood that not all her experiences and communications were from God. “Sometimes I must talk and think about the everyday things of life. I must write letters or give information to some workers. These words and information do not come from God.”—Selected Messages, book 1, page 39, adapted. We must remember this impor­tant idea when we read her writings, experiences, letters, and diaries. Then we will not misunderstand her words or use her writing to say something she didn’t mean.
