### Bible Worker of Hope

In January 1906, a young Slovenian man named Albin Močnik stood on a bridge in Bremerhaven, Germany. Albin thought about jumping off the bridge and killing himself. But a stranger stopped him. The stranger introduced him to a pair of Seventh-day Adventist families. The names of the families were the Schiefers and the Banas. Both families were shoemakers.

Albin moved in with the Schiefers and the Banas. Their quiet and loving lives helped Albin to accept Jesus. In March 1906, Albin was baptized.

Soon after Albin’s baptism, he went to a meeting in Friedensau. Albin didn’t know that he was the first Slovenian Seventh-day Adventist when he went there. Albin decided to go to Friedensau school so that he could serve his own people better. Soon, Albin started to translate Sabbath School lessons into the Serbo-Croatian language.

In 1908, Albin finished his studies. He went to Croatia with Pastor Robert Schilliner. Albin shared the Good News about Jesus in Zagreb and in Split. Then Albin moved into Srem. Srem was a part of Croatia at that time. After that, Albin entered Serbia. There, he preached and baptized new members. He even got locked in jail for preaching.

Albin was often sick. But he never gave up. During World War I, Albin served in the army. His health was poor. He was sad that he was away from his wife and children. While he rested to get well, he printed the first Seventh-day Adventist hymnbook in the Serbo-Croatian language. He even wrote 120 songs himself.

Albin helped heal the German Seventh-day Adventist church to heal after some members tried to break it apart. Before World War I, some church members in Germany disagreed about fighting in the war. Some Germans said it was wrong. Others disagreed. Albin helped to unite the church after the war. Soon, Albin’s work for God in his own country was a success. He gave the church new life. He chose new leaders. In 1925, He became the first president of the Yugoslav Union Conference.

As president, Albin did many things to help the church. In 1925, his book Adventism helped government leaders understand his church’s beliefs. He edited the church paper Glasnik. He also started a school for pastors in 1931.

Albin suffered much, but his faith was strong. When he chose life and not death on that bridge in Bremerhaven, he became a Bible worker of hope. His story shows that the Good News about Jesus can change lives. Albin had the courage to share the Good News about Jesus in new places.
