---
title:  Written Messages
date:   28/10/2026
---

Some famous sayings and talks have changed people’s lives and human history. But if these words weren’t written down, no one would remember them. Or if people do remember, they may not remember the correct words or ideas. So, writing helps us remember what people say. If they write down their words, we can read what they wrote whenever we want.

Shortly after the Israelites left Egypt, they won a fight against the Amalekites. God told Moses to “ ‘write about this battle. Write these things in a book so that people will remember what happened here. And be sure to tell Joshua that I will completely destroy the Amalekites from the earth’ ” (Exodus 17:14, ERV). The Holy Spirit helped Moses to write the first five books of the Bible, the book of Job, and Psalm 90. Then the Holy Spirit helped God’s messengers to write the rest of the 66 books that now are part of the Bible.

`Why did God ask His messengers to write down the messages He gave them? Read Exodus 17:14; John 20:30, 31; 1 Corinthians 10:11; and Revelation 1:19 for the answer.`

The different books of the Bible show us the different personalities and writing skills of the men who wrote them. As we will learn next week, their different personalities and writing skills don’t make some parts of the Bible more important than other parts. Yes, some parts may have more meaning for our lives and faith today. But that doesn’t make one part of the Bible more holy than another part.

Sometimes Bible writers wrote God’s messages after they first shared the messages with people. Sometimes an assistant wrote down the messenger’s words. In the book of Deuteronomy, Moses wrote the final sermons that he preached to the Israelites before he died. The first four books of the New Testament contain many sermons and teachings from Jesus. Sometimes, New Testament writers used quotes from outside the Old Testament (Acts 17:28; 1 Corinthians 15:33; Titus 1:12). Some Bible writers also wrote down the words of Satan (Genesis 3:1–5; Matthew 4:1–11).

When we read the Bible, we should remember that the Bible writers were not perfect. So, their writings are not written in perfect language. Even so, the Bible’s message is truth. We can trust forever what it says.
