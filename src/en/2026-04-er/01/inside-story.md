### The Start of the SDA Church in Poland

In 1888, Poland was controlled by other countries. At this time, a new spiritual light appeared in Poland. Herman Szkubowiec became the first Seventh-day Adventist in Poland. Later, Herman started the first Seventh-day Adventist church in Poland.

In 1893, two Polish men became Seventh-day Adventists. These men were Karol Fendel and Jozef Szlodzinksi. Karol and Jozef traveled to the city of Łódź. There, Karol and Jozef shared their faith with a Baptist family named Lach. The Lachs ­accepted the Seventh-day Adventist message. They held Sabbath worship in their home. Sharing new religious ideas in Poland wasn’t easy at that time. Most Polish people didn’t trust people who weren’t from Poland. But the Seventh-day Adventist message started to grow in the Lach’s house church. In 1895, a new church group started in Łódź. A Russian pastor named Henrich Loebsack held public meetings. Pastor Loebsack baptized the first Seventh-day Adventist members in the city.

Another pastor named Herbert Schmitz felt that God wanted him to preach in Warsaw. Pastor Schmitz was a German who did not speak the Polish language. He looked for houses in Warsaw with German names on the doors. When the owners welcomed him in, he explained the Seventh-day Adventist faith. His small audience was mostly German. But the Seventh-day Adventist faith started to grow in that community. In a few months, Pastor Schmitz started a church in Warsaw. Soon, sermons were preached in both German and Polish.

In 1912, Schmitz became the first leader for the growing work in Poland. Even after World War I started, the Seventh-day Adventist Church in Poland continued to grow. In 1921, the Seventh-day Adventist Union in Poland was started. The Seventh-day Adventists in Poland started to build their churches again after the war. At that time, they had 1,080 members. The churches continued to grow. Soon Bible meetings were held. Bible workers sold books about Jesus across Poland. Seventh-day Adventist schools were started.

The most amazing part of this story is those early Bible workers. These workers knocked on doors. They preached in private homes. They baptized new members in secret. In 1988, the Seventh-day Adventist Church celebrated its work in Poland. The small group that met in the Lach family home was now a strong church across the whole country. The courage of the first members helps us remember that the light of Bible truth shines bright in dark places.
