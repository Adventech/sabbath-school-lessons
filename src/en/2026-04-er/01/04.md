---
title:  God Comes Looking For Us
date:   29/09/2026
---

Genesis 3:9, 10 teaches us that God goes looking for His lost children. God makes the first move. He doesn’t wait for us to come back to Him first. Look at God’s question to Adam, “ ‘Where are you?’ ” (Genesis 3:9, ERV). Did God need to ask this question? Of course not! God knew where Adam and Eve hid. He also knew what they did. So, why then does God ask this question? Because sin cut off communication between God and Adam and Eve. God asks the question because He wants to communicate with His children again. God wants to meet with them and give them hope. That’s why “the Lord God called to the man. The Lord said, ‘Where are you?’ ” (Genesis 3:9). Do you see the word “called”? In the Hebrew language, this word often shows us God’s wish to start communicating with His people.

`Read the story in Luke 15:11–32. How does it help us better understand what happened in Genesis 3:9, 10?`

The Lord goes looking for Adam and Eve because He loves them. God never gives up on us (read Luke 15), even when we hide from Him. Jesus will do everything He can to bring us back to His Father. Before Jesus died, three times Peter acted as if he didn’t know Jesus. Then Jesus “turned and looked” (Luke 22:61, NKJV) at Peter. Jesus’ look cut Peter’s heart. With that look, Jesus tried to bring Peter back to God.

Notice in Genesis 3:11–13 that sin changed Adam and Eve’s relationship with God. At first, Adam and Eve didn’t confess their sin or guilt. They blamed each other. First, Adam blamed Eve. Next, Adam blamed God for giving him Eve (Genesis 3:12)! Then Eve blamed the snake (Genesis 3:13). Not much has changed with us in 6,000 years, right?

For sure, sin has hurt all of us. Paul explains the sin problem in Romans 7:15–24. He talks about the pain he feels when he wants to do what is right but then does bad things: “I do not do the good things that I want to do. Instead [but], I do the things that I hate to do” (Romans 7:15, EASY).

Everyone feels this way. Who hasn’t?

Paul’s words are powerful. We are all broken people. We keep doing things we know we shouldn’t do. Our only hope is Jesus (Romans 7:25). God continues to look for us so He can lead us to Jesus. Only Jesus can save us from sin.
