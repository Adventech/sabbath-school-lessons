God doesn’t limit His communication with people to Bible times. As we will see in coming weeks, God talks to people in every age. God will communicate His words to us in modern ways until Jesus comes back.

Ellen Gould Harmon White and her twin sister Elizabeth were born on November 26, 1827, in Gorham, Maine. They were the youngest of eight children born to Robert and Eunice Harmon. In a few years, the family moved to the city of Portland, Maine, where Robert Harmon hoped to build his hat-making business. At about the age of 9, Ellen suffered a terrible injury. While she was in a park near her home, an angry classmate threw a stone at her. The stone hit Ellen in the face. For many weeks after, Ellen was very sick in bed. Because of this accident, Ellen stopped going to school. She had health problems for the rest of her life. She also felt bad about the way she looked at the time. So, she didn’t visit many people.

Sick and injured, young Ellen started to think about her spiritual future. She was afraid of God. She didn’t feel worthy of going to heaven. Her feelings grew worse after the Harmon family accepted William Miller’s teaching that Jesus was coming back soon. Ellen was baptized into the Methodist Church, in 1842. But she didn’t feel worthy of God’s love. She believed she was “forever lost.”—Life Sketches of Ellen G. White, page 29; also read pages 20–31.

During this time, young Ellen had two dreams. The dreams changed her ideas about God. Later, she met a Methodist preacher named Levi Stockman. Stockman helped Ellen understand God’s love. She wrote later, “Faith now filled my heart. I felt a love for God that I couldn’t put into words. I felt my sins were forgiven. My ideas about the Father changed. I now saw Him as a loving parent, and not a strict master who forced people to obey Him without question. My heart filled with deep love for Him.”—Life Sketches of Ellen G. White, page 39, adapted.

During this time, Ellen learned the Bible teaching about what happens when we die. She also learned that there was no forever-burning hell. Her new understanding about God changed her whole life. We will learn each Friday about how, Ellen G. White fell deeply in love with Jesus. She served Him for the rest of her life.
