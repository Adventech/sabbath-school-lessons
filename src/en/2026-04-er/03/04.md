---
title:  Loyal To God
date:   13/10/2026
---

`Read 1 Samuel 8:4–22 and Leviticus 8:10–13. Many of God’s messengers worked with kings and religious leaders. How did God’s messengers help these leaders?`

At times, God’s messengers worked with kings. Sometimes their experiences were good. Other times, their experiences were awful. God’s messenger Samuel had a terrible experience when Israel asked for a king (1 Samuel 8:4–22). This sad story shows us an important lesson. In every age, God’s people must be careful not to want to be the same as the people around them (1 Samuel 8:5). In the story in 1 Samuel 8, the people didn’t really understand the terrible things that would happen when they got a king (1 Samuel 8:11–18). Of course, God told Samuel to warn His people. But the people wanted a king anyway. So, soon Samuel poured oil on the head of Saul to show that God chose him. Then Saul became Israel’s first king.

In the Old Testament, the books of 1 and 2 Kings and 1 and 2 Chronicles tell us about the lives of Israel’s kings. More kings did evil than good. One job of God’s messengers was to help evil kings come back to God and obey Him. Many of these kings hurt or killed God’s messengers. These evil kings caused God’s people to break His law (1 Kings 18:4–19; 2 Chronicles 24:20–22).

Many of God’s messengers also worked with Israel’s religious leaders. Moses is an example of one of these messengers. Moses poured oil on Aaron and his sons to show that God chose them as Israel’s first religious leaders (Leviticus 8:10–13). Sometimes God used His messengers to warn the religious leaders (read Hosea 6:9; Malachi 2:1–3). A powerful example is when the man of God warns Eli about his evil sons (1 Samuel 2:27–36).

God’s messengers were also teachers (2 Chronicles 34). God’s messengers announced His messages to the religious leaders and kings, too (Jeremiah 5:31; Ezekiel 22:26). Sometimes God’s messengers also told the leaders of foreign countries about God’s plans. We can read some of these messages in the Old Testament (Isaiah 60:1–3; Isaiah 62:1, 2). Sometimes God asked His messengers to write down His messages (2 Chronicles 13:22). God’s messengers were men and women of prayer. They obeyed God’s commands. They served Him and His people with their whole hearts (Jeremiah 42:4; 2 Kings 22:15–17).
