---
title:  Not Perfect, But Willing To Serve
date:   16/10/2026
---

As we saw already, being God’s messenger is never easy. The lives of the Old Testament messengers show us this truth. Ellen G. White also felt the same fears and worries that the Bible messengers felt. As we learned last Friday, Ellen Harmon [White] was young, shy, and in poor health when God chose her to be His messenger. God gave her a second dream as proof that He chose her. But she was worried about being God’s messenger. She wrote:

“About a week later, God gave me another dream. He showed me the troubles I must suffer. I must go and tell other people what He showed me. People would try to stop me. I would suffer much. . . . This dream upset me very much. My health was poor. I was only 17 years old. . . . I prayed that God would choose someone else. But He continued to say to me, ‘Tell other people what I showed you.’ I didn’t want to go out in public. I was very shy. . . . I wanted to die. Then I wouldn’t have to do the things God asked me to do.”—Spiritual Gifts, volume 2, pages 35, 36, adapted.

Do you see that Ellen Harmon [White] said she wanted to die? Then she wouldn’t need to be God’s messenger. Ellen Harmon [White] prayed long and hard. Her friends encouraged her. Finally, she ­accepted the work God gave her. For the rest of her life, she served God. She gave her life to the Lord “to do everything He asked me to do.”—Ellen G. White, Spiritual Gifts, volume 2, page 38.

Ellen G. White’s writings became powerful in the lives of church members, then and now. But Ellen White was human. She had her own personal problems. She was often sick. She had family problems, too, at times. Her life teaches us an important lesson: God chooses people who aren’t perfect but who are willing to serve Him.

The Holy Spirit helped Ellen G. White to write down God’s messages. Ellen White’s writings encourage church members and connect them with Bible truth. Ellen White helped the Seventh-day Adventist people to become a church, in 1863. In later years, Ellen White’s writings helped start the worldwide health work and many schools. For sure, the most important thing that she gave the church was her writings. She wrote nearly 100,000 pages. Ellen G. White’s writings continue to help people worldwide today.
