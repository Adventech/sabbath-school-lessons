---
title:  God’s Real Messengers
date:   12/10/2026
---

In the early history of Israel, God warned His people against false messengers or prophets (Deuteronomy 18:20). God also helped His people to know who His real messengers were.

God’s prophets announced God’s messages, even if their messages upset people in power.

`Read 1 Kings 22:1–14. What does this verse tell us about God’s messengers and how they behaved?`

King Ahab hated Micaiah. Micaiah was God’s messenger. King Ahab said, “ ‘I hate him because he will not say anything good about me when he speaks for the Lord’ ” (1 Kings 22:8, ERV). These words tell us a lot about Ahab. (He was not a very good person, was he?) The king’s words tell us even more about Micaiah. He was not afraid to say the truth, even to the king.

Micaiah stood alone against 400 false messengers. He announced the Bible truth that God gave to him to share. King Ahab tried to force Micaiah to change his message (1 Kings 22:13, 14). But Micaiah refused to change God’s word. He stayed loyal to God and the work He gave him to do.

`Read 1 Samuel 9:3–14. What do these verses tell us about what other people thought about God’s messengers?`

In 1 Samuel 9:3–14, Kish sends Saul, his son, to look for his lost donkeys. Saul’s servant says to him, “ ‘There is a servant of God who lives in this town. Everything that he says will happen really happens. So people respect him very much. We should go to meet him. Perhaps [Maybe] he will tell us which way we should go from here’ ” (1 Samuel 9:6, EASY). The servant is talking about Samuel, God’s messenger. The servant’s words help us understand that people expected God’s messengers to be honest and loyal to God. God chose His messengers to show His love. At the same time, God’s messengers are human. So, they are not perfect at all.
