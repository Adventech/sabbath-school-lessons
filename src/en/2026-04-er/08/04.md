---
title:  The Words Around A Word Or Bible Verse
date:   17/11/2026
---

Preachers tell the story about a man who suffered deeply. The man wanted to know God’s plan for his life. So, the man opened his Bible and put his finger on a verse. He read: “So Judas took the money and he threw it down on the floor in the temple. Then he went away. He hung himself from a rope so that he died” (Matthew 27:5, EASY). The sad man was surprised. Did God really want him to hang himself? The man opened the Bible again to be sure. He then read, “Jesus said to him, ‘Then go and do the same thing he did!’ ” (Luke 10:37, ICB). For sure, both verses are Bible truth, but they are talking about separate things. We need to understand the stories and chapters connected to the verses we are studying. Then we can better understand the real meaning of those verses.

Many people don’t understand the Bible because they do what the very sad man did. They try to understand a verse without carefully studying the words around it. If we don’t study those words, we can’t understand the Bible correctly.

`Read Satan’s words in Genesis 3:1–5 and Matthew 4:1–11. How did Satan give God’s words a wrong ­meaning?`

Some people teach false ideas about the Bible because they really don’t know any better. Other people know Bible truth. But they teach false ideas, the same as Satan did. In the Garden of Eden, Satan came to Eve as a beautiful talking snake! (Compare Genesis 3:1–5 with Revelation 12:9.) Satan gave God’s words a false meaning.

When the Holy Spirit led Jesus into the desert (Matthew 4:1–11; Luke 4:1–13), “Satan came to trick Jesus. Satan pretended to be an angel from heaven.”—Ellen G. White, in The Advent Review and Sabbath Herald, December 18, 1888, adapted. In Satan’s conversation with Jesus, Satan changed the meaning of God’s words in Psalm 91:11, 12. Satan told Jesus that God would protect people who put their own lives in danger. As we can see, Satan tried to make these verses say something that God didn’t promise.

`If we want to understand a word or a verse, we must first try to understand the verses around it. We also must try to understand the story or chapter it comes from. Why is this advice so important?`
