`In Isaiah 65:17–20, we read about the new heavens and earth. What does Isaiah mean when he says that “the child will die one hundred years old” there (Isaiah 65:20, WEB)?`

Is Isaiah really saying that there will be death and sinners in heaven and on the new earth? Of course not! This idea goes against the promise in Isaiah that God will wake up His people from the dead (Isaiah 26:19). This idea also goes against the promise that there won’t be any more death or sorrow (Isaiah 25:8; Isaiah 65:19).

So, then, how should we understand Isaiah 65:20? First, we must understand that Isaiah is using symbols. When did you see a 100-year-old child? Never, right? Second, Isaiah also uses the beautiful language of poems to help us understand the blessings God will give Israel. But Israel must obey God to get these blessings (compare with Deuteronomy 28). Isaiah does the same thing that Jesus does in Matthew 24. Jesus connected things that were happening in His day with things that would happen later. In Isaiah 65:17–20, Isaiah explains that the things happening in his time showed the things that will happen in the new heavens and new earth.

In the book of Isaiah, the new life is not something that happens only in the future. Isaiah 65:20 announces that God is already destroying death. God makes us new, right here and now. He saves us and forgives us. God also promises us long life and no more death (Isaiah 65:20). These promises show us the wonderful things to come.

`Read 1 Corinthians 16:20. What does Paul say to do in this verse? How do we follow this advice today?`

In New Testament times, close friends and family greeted each other with a kiss. Paul tells Christians to greet each other this way, too (Romans 16:16; 1 Corinthians 16:20; 2 Corinthians 13:12; and 1 Thessalonians 5:26). Paul helps us to understand that Christians must greet each other warmly. They must make each other feel welcome and part of the family of God. In some countries today, kissing is not an accepted greeting. But a handshake, a hug, or a bow would be fine. The important idea is that Paul wants us to make church members feel accepted and loved whenever we meet and get together.
